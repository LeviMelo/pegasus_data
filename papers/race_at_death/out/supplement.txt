Supplementary material - The race on the death certificate as a classification of place: the mother's declaration, the family's classification and the certified race of children and young people across 5,570 Brazilian municipalities, 2013-2024

Supplementary Text S1 - Estimation without individual linkage and its control

Let G be a group of deaths. For a death i, let C_i be the set of live births with the birth date, sex and municipality of residence of i. If the family did not move, exactly one element of C_i is the deceased; the remaining elements, and all elements when the family moved, are births that agree by chance. Write N(j, k) for the number of deaths in G with race j on the birth record and certified race k, and t for the probability that the deceased is in C_i. The expected number of candidates with race j among deaths certified k is t N(j, k) + E(j, k), where E(j, k) is the expected number of chance candidates.

E(j, k) depends on how many children were born on the birth date of the deceased in the municipality. That is not the number of an arbitrary day: a day on which more children were born contributes more people and therefore more deaths, so the birth date of a death is a size-biased draw from the days of the year, and a control drawn from other days would undercount E. The births of the other sex on the same day in the same municipality share the day, cannot contain the deceased and have the racial composition of that day's births; multiplied by the ratio of births of the two sexes in the municipality and year, their count estimates E(j, k). With this control the estimated t for infants was 89.8% overall (Supplementary Table S2).

The estimator of t N(j, k) is the difference of the two counts. Its variance was approximated by the sum of the candidate count and the control count, which ignores the scaling factor of the control (close to 1). A proportion is a ratio of two such differences; the variance of its denominator was ignored, which understates the error of rows with few deaths. A twin of the same sex is a candidate and a twin of the other sex is in the control; both share the mother's race with the deceased. Mortality by the mother's race uses the shares of each race among the estimated deaths, so that t cancels provided it does not differ by race.

Supplementary Text S2 - Three readings of race for the same woman

Deaths of women aged 15 to 49 years in 2015 to 2022 in municipalities with fewer than 2,000 mothers on the birth records (106,565 deaths) were related to the birth records on which they were the mother, by birth date and municipality (19,332 pairs). These deaths were then related to the hospital admission that ended in death, through a probabilistic linkage of admissions in the Hospital Information System to death certificates on birth date, sex, date of death and hospital. 5,579 women had the three races recorded; the mean estimated proportion of false pairs among them was 28.2%. False pairs were removed in expectation, taking the declared race of a false pair to follow the racial composition of mothers in the region; intervals are from 400 bootstrap resamples of the women. The certificate recorded the race the woman had declared as a mother for 0.80 (95%CI 0.77; 0.82) of White, 0.71 (95%CI 0.69; 0.73) of Brown and 0.55 (95%CI 0.48; 0.63) of Black women; the hospital record did so for 0.76 (95%CI 0.73; 0.78), 0.77 (95%CI 0.75; 0.79) and 0.37 (95%CI 0.30; 0.44) (Supplementary Table S8). For comparison, among 1,208,430 delivery admissions of 2022 related to the birth record, the hospital recorded the declared race for 0.74 of White, 0.80 of Brown and 0.37 of Black mothers.

Supplementary Text S3 - Birth cohorts at three censuses (exploratory)

The racial composition of five-year birth cohorts at the censuses of 2000, 2010 and 2022 was read, by single year of age and race, from a cohort-component population account of every municipality built for this research programme from the censuses, the Mortality and Live Birth Information Systems and the official estimates [41]; in a census year the account takes the census population as given, so the shares at the three dates are census shares. The account is not a published product and its census-year values carry the censuses' own coverage error, which is not quantified by race. A cohort's White share can fall between censuses through reclassification, but also through higher mortality or emigration of White people, through differences in coverage between censuses and through the change of the 2022 questionnaire; the comparison between the first census of a cohort aged 0-4 and later ones also compares a race reported for small children by an adult of the household with one reported for adults. The analysis describes the cohorts and does not separate these explanations.

Supplementary Table S16 - Weight of the local distribution (λ) in a mixture model of the certified race of children, by mother's race, age and reference distribution, Brazil

C(m -> c) = (1 - λ) F(m -> c) + λ π(c) over ten classes of municipal composition, F the family's classification in the census and π the local reference. λ = 0: the certificate records the family's classification; λ = 1: it records the local distribution whatever the mother's race. With the certified race of local deaths as the reference, the reference contains the certificates being modelled, which biases λ upward; the fit for Black mothers is poor under both references.
