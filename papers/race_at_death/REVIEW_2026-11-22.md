# Review of the six race papers, 2026-11-22

By the coordinating session, on the versions of commit 34f524ff. Read in full: the manuscript text of papers 1 to 5,
the abstract, outline and Discussion of paper 6, the handover note, paper 1's display plan, and six figures opened as
images. Not read: the supplements, the audits and responses, the code. No number was recomputed. This is a review of
the science, the argument and the display items; the audits have covered the arithmetic.

## 1. Verdict

The set is now real work. The papers state rival readings and test them, report results that went against their own
hypotheses (paper 5 reversed its conclusion; paper 1's long panel is called uninformative), and the figures are
composites with flow diagrams and forests. Ranked by how close each is to a submission:

## 2. Across the papers

**A. The reference itself is unstable, and only paper 3 says so.** Paper 3 finds that a mother who declared herself
Black at one birth does so again at the next in 0.52 of cases (0.87 Brown, 0.75 White). Papers 1 and 2 take the
mother's declaration as a fixed class. Consequences to work through:
- Paper 2's Black to Brown ratio of 1.15 and the "8,543 excess deaths" of Black mothers' infants are computed on a
  class half of whose members would be in another class at another birth. Give the ratio under the misclassification
  the repeat births measure (a sensitivity with the 3 x 3 repeat matrix), and for Black and Brown combined.
- Paper 1's "0.18 certified Black" and paper 3's whole ladder should be read against 0.52, not against 1 (3.1).

**B. Is the mother's race on the birth record itself a product of place?** Papers 1 and 2 list it as a limitation.
It can be tested with data in hand: the share of White (and Black) among SINASC mothers of 2021-2023 against the
share among women aged 15-49 in the 2022 Census, by municipality, where race is self-declared to an interviewer. If
the two agree along the whole range of composition, the birth record is not bending toward the local majority and
the gradient belongs to the certificate. If the birth record is itself pulled, paper 1's gradient is understated or
overstated by a measurable amount. Paper 1 used the census share only as a substitute covariate (correlation 0.94);
the test is the slope and the departure from the diagonal, not the correlation.

**C. One rival of paper 1 can be tested in paper 4's data.** "The Brown category is not the same population
everywhere" predicts that children of the same couple type are reported lighter in the South. The 2010 Census has the
municipality: the share of children of a Brown mother and a Brown father reported White, by municipal composition
and by state. If it is flat, families do not carry a regional Brown category and the certificate's gradient is the
certifier's. If it rises, part of the certificate's gradient is the category. Paper 1's family map mixes this with
the partner mix, which also varies by region; paper 4 can separate them. This is the most valuable analysis not yet
run in the set.

**D. Space is still uneven.** Paper 1 is spatial throughout and paper 2 has regional maps. Paper 3 has region as a
stratum, paper 5 as a supplementary table, paper 4 as five odds ratios. The author's standing instruction is that
every study uses place and time as dimensions. Each of 3, 4 and 5 needs at least one main panel that shows where:
hospital practice by state or health region (3), the family's classification by state for fixed couple types in both
censuses (4), the cause contrast by region and by the composition of the place of occurrence (5).

**E. References.** Paper 4 has 7 and paper 5 has 8. Neither engages its literature: for 4, interracial unions and the
classification of children, reclassification between censuses, the incentives after the 2012 quota law; for 5,
Brazilian work on the race of homicide victims, on the quality of race on death certificates of external causes and
on medico-legal certification. Search, read and verify each reference before citing; do not cite from memory.

**F. Overlap between papers.** The same numbers (0.76/0.64/0.18, the linkage, the estimator) open papers 1, 2 and 3,
and the estimator is described in five. Each paper must say in one sentence what the companions report and not
repeat it as its own result. Whether six papers or fewer go out, and to which journals, is the author's decision;
prepare for it a one-page map of which result is reported where.

**G. Process text in a manuscript.** Paper 6's Discussion says a reference "could not be verified" because Crossref
does not index the journal. That is a note for the authors, not text for readers (6.1).

## 3. Paper by paper

### Paper 1, place

1.1 **Run test C above** and report it in the readings table, row "The Brown category differs between regions",
which is now "not settled" on a comparison the text calls inconclusive.

1.2 **Run test B above** and move limitation (3) from a caveat to a result.

1.3 **Length and readability.** The "Time and place" paragraph carries about forty numbers and three designs; a
reader cannot follow it. Keep in the text one sentence per design with its estimate, and what the three together
allow to be said; move the rest to a supplementary table that Figure 5 already summarises. The same for "Who
classifies": the investigation categories and the physician variance each need two sentences. Target 4,500 words.

1.4 **Title and scope.** The title says "infants and children ... 2013-2024"; children after infancy are one
paragraph. Either the title names infants, or the 1-9 years analysis gets a figure panel.

1.5 **Abstract.** The Results are one block of about 250 words ending in a sentence few will parse ("as often as not
where 58% of births were White, not half"). State four findings in four sentences.

1.6 **The establishment.** The crossed model puts the residual variance in the establishment (0.29 against 0.03 for
the municipality), and the physician model puts it in the physician. These are the paper's most concrete findings
about who classifies, and they are absent from the abstract and the conclusion, which still speak of the
municipality and the state. Say at which level the classification varies.

1.7 Figure 4A: the legend lies on the points. Figure 1: the census box is connected to nothing; draw how it enters.

### Paper 2, indicators

2.1 **Scaling of the unlinked deaths.** Linked deaths are scaled to all certificates by certified race only, while
the linked share is 76% in the North and 89% in the South, and 65% after the first month. The mother's-race
distribution of the scaled deaths is therefore tilted toward the South and toward neonatal deaths. Post-stratify the
scaling by region, age at death and certified race (and year), report how the four ratios and the excess deaths
move, and make the finer scaling the main analysis if they move.

2.2 **Anchor "the usual way".** The paper's claim is about a rate others compute. Reproduce one published official
or academic figure of infant mortality by race for the same years with the paper's own "usual" computation, cite
it, and show the two side by side. Without it a reader cannot tell whether anyone computes the rate this way.

2.3 **How much of 0.80 is region.** By the mother's race the ratio is 0.80 crude, 0.87 standardised to the regions,
and 0.84 to 0.96 inside each region. The abstract gives only the crude value, and the 44,851 excess deaths are
computed on it. Report the standardised ratio in the abstract and the excess deaths within regions.

2.4 **Births with no race** are left out of every denominator while deaths are scaled to all certificates. Say what
share of births that is by year and what it does to the rates (levels, not ratios).

2.5 **Black to Brown**: see A.

2.6 Figure 3A is described in the text as "mostly the prior". A panel that shows the prior should not be a main
panel; show the regions' estimates with intervals ranked, or the five macro-regions.

### Paper 3, Black category

3.1 **The benchmark.** The headline compares the infant's certificate (0.18) and the mother's hospital record (0.36)
with the mother's declaration taken as 1. But the paper's own data give the right comparators: the same mother
declares Black again in 0.52; families report Black mothers' infants as Black in 0.39 to 0.44; the infant is not the
mother. Against those, the hospital "loses" 0.52 to 0.36 and the certificate 0.39-0.44 to 0.18. Rebuild the ladder
figure and the abstract around these four levels, each named for what it is. The title's "recorded as Brown" then
describes the certificate against the family, which is the defensible claim.

3.2 **The same hospital fills both records.** The birth record's "declaration" and the admission's race are both
written in the delivery hospital, and they disagree widely (0.01 to 0.64 of declared-Black mothers recorded Black on
the admission, by hospital). Say who fills each form and when, from the manuals, and whether hospitals with low
admission shares also have low shares of mothers declared Black on the birth record given their municipality's
census composition. If they do, the "declaration" in those hospitals is not the mother's.

3.3 **Where.** Hospital practice by state or health region, as a map or ranked plot, belongs in a main figure.

3.4 The comparison is limited to public-system deliveries (53-61% of births); say what kind of mothers that leaves out.

### Paper 4, family

4.1 **No space.** See C and D. In 2010 the municipality is available; in 2022 the state. Show the couple-type shares
by state in both censuses, and the municipal gradient in 2010.

4.2 **"Both parents count about alike" is not what the numbers say.** Brown mother with White father: White in
0.56. White mother with Brown father: White in 0.61. The child takes the mother's class in 0.44 of the first and
0.61 of the second. That is a pull toward White plus a parent effect, not symmetry. Fit the nine couple types with a
mother term, a father term and a category term, and state each.

4.3 **Table 1** prints model terms as they left the software ("sex: 2", "mother_age: 35-44"), two outcomes stacked
in one long column. Rebuild it as two columns of odds ratios with labelled rows grouped by variable.

4.4 **Findings stated and not discussed**: girls are reported White more often than boys (odds ratio 1.10) and
Black less often (0.89); older children of Brown mothers are reported White less often. Either discuss them with the
literature on gender and colour or leave them in the supplement.

4.5 **The quota law of 2012** changed the incentives to declare between the two censuses. It is the obvious
contextual explanation for change among older children and is not mentioned.

### Paper 5, violence

5.1 **Place of occurrence.** Paper 1 shows the certificate moves with the composition of the municipality where the
death occurs. Residents of small municipalities who die of external causes may die, or be certified, somewhere else
than those who die of natural causes: on a road, in a regional hospital, at a medico-legal institute in a larger
city. The paper standardises to the composition of the municipality of residence only. Compare the causes by
whether the death occurred in the municipality of residence, and standardise to the composition of the place of
occurrence. If the cause contrast survives, it is a finding about the death; if not, it is paper 1's finding again.

5.2 **The informant.** Natural deaths at 15-24 mostly follow illness, with family at the hospital; external deaths
are certified from the body. The contrast "attending physician against medical examiner" within assaults has an
interval of 0.24 to 0.56 and settles nothing. Use the place of death (hospital after admission of some days,
against the scene or on arrival) as the proxy for a family informant, within external causes.

5.3 **Carry paper 6's limits.** Half the deaths are not found, and paper 6 shows that who is found depends on the
class recorded. State what differential finding by race at birth would do to the cause contrast, with the size
paper 6's simulation gives.

5.4 The Population paragraph has a broken sentence ("Municipalities natural causes are all other codes") and
defines natural causes twice; Table 2 prints "-0".

5.5 The comparison group: natural deaths at 15-24 are few and unusual (47% had an earlier admission against 21%).
Say what they are (cause groups) and repeat the main contrast against transport accidents as the reference, which
the data already suggest behaves like assaults.

### Paper 6, estimator

6.1 **Compare the closest published method.** The Discussion names a maximum-likelihood method for contingency
tables from incorrectly linked data and says it was not compared because the reference could not be verified in
Crossref. Find the paper at its publisher (the journal is published by Statistics Canada and is online), read it,
cite it properly, and either implement it as a comparator or say precisely why it does not apply. Remove the
sentence about Crossref from the manuscript.

6.2 **Validation by age is in hand.** The admissions-to-certificates validation covers all ages. Stratify it by age
band (under 1, 1-4, 5-14, 15-24, older) and report the difference and the interval misses for each. That answers
"not validated for ages 1-14" for hospital deaths, which is most of what can be answered, and it serves papers 1,
2, 3 and 5, which all use the estimator after infancy.

6.3 **The working limit** is the expected number of chance candidates per unit, as the Limitations say; define the
estimator's domain by that quantity and show accuracy against it in one panel, instead of by municipality size
class with a threshold chosen on the validation data.

6.4 The abstract is over 400 words and the main tables run to hundreds of printed rows. A methods paper needs one
table of the validation and one figure of the simulation in the text; the rest is supplement.
