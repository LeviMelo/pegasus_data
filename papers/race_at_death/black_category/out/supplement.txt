Supplementary material - Black children recorded as Brown: the Black category on the death certificates of children of Black mothers, against the family, the hospital and the certificates of adults, Brazil, 2013-2023
Supplementary Text S1 - Three readings of race for the same woman (exploratory)
Deaths of women aged 15 to 49 years in 2015 to 2022 in municipalities with fewer than 2,000 mothers on the birth records (106,565 deaths) were related to the birth records on which they were the mother, by birth date and municipality (19,332 pairs). These deaths were then related to the hospital admission that ended in death, through a probabilistic linkage of admissions in the Hospital Information System to death certificates on birth date, sex, date of death and hospital. 5,579 women had the three races recorded; the mean estimated proportion of false pairs among them was 28.2%. False pairs were removed in expectation, taking the declared race of a false pair to follow the racial composition of mothers in the region; intervals are from 400 bootstrap resamples of the women. The certificate recorded the race the woman had declared as a mother for 0.80 (95%CI 0.77; 0.82) of White, 0.71 (95%CI 0.69; 0.73) of Brown and 0.55 (95%CI 0.48; 0.63) of Black women; the hospital record did so for 0.76 (95%CI 0.73; 0.78), 0.77 (95%CI 0.75; 0.79) and 0.37 (95%CI 0.31; 0.44) (Supplementary Table S1). For comparison, among 1,208,430 delivery admissions of 2022 related to the birth record, the hospital recorded the declared race for 0.74 of White, 0.80 of Brown and 0.37 of Black mothers.
Supplementary Text S2 - Estimation without individual linkage and its control
Let G be a group of deaths. For a death i, let C_i be the set of live births with the birth date, sex and municipality of residence of i. If the family did not move, exactly one element of C_i is the deceased; the remaining elements, and all elements when the family moved, are births that agree by chance. Write N(j, k) for the number of deaths in G with race j on the birth record and certified race k, and t for the probability that the deceased is in C_i. The expected number of candidates with race j among deaths certified k is t N(j, k) + E(j, k), where E(j, k) is the expected number of chance candidates.
E(j, k) depends on how many children were born on the birth date of the deceased in the municipality. That is not the number of an arbitrary day: a day on which more children were born contributes more people and therefore more deaths, so the birth date of a death is a size-biased draw from the days of the year, and a control drawn from other days would undercount E. The births of the other sex on the same day in the same municipality share the day, cannot contain the deceased and have the racial composition of that day's births; multiplied by the ratio of births of the two sexes in the municipality and year, their count estimates E(j, k). With this control the estimated t for infants was 89.8% overall.
The estimator of t N(j, k) is the difference of the two counts. Its variance was approximated by the sum of the candidate count and the control count, which ignores the scaling factor of the control (close to 1). A proportion is a ratio of two such differences; the variance of its denominator was ignored, which understates the error of rows with few deaths. A twin of the same sex is a candidate and a twin of the other sex is in the control; both share the mother's race with the deceased. Mortality by the mother's race uses the shares of each race among the estimated deaths, so that t cancels provided it does not differ by race.
