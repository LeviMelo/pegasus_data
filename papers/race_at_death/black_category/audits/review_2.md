# Review 2 - paper 3, `black_category/` (referee standard: SS&M / IJE / Demography)

Read: manuscript.txt, supplement.txt, black.json, black.py (facility model), counts.py (delivery job), audit 1 and its response, abstracts of papers 1 and 2 (place, indicators), the data manual (`pegasus-data info`, columns SINASC.DN, SIM.DO). Nothing run, nothing edited.

## 0. Overall judgement: MAJOR REVISION

The descriptive finding is real and important: three health records classify the same Black women and their infants as Black at 0.36, 0.28 (blanks counted) and 0.18, and the net certificate ratio is 0.34. Audit 1's arithmetic repairs hold. The paper does not yet answer its stated question, "by whom" a Black person is recorded as Black, for three reasons.

1. **The reference is not a reference.** The mother's "declaration" on the birth record is entered by hospital staff (SINASC `TPFUNCRESP` says who), usually the same hospital and day as the SIH admission it is compared with. The paper therefore compares two staff records of one woman made in the same ward within hours. A 64% disagreement between them is the finding, and "Black recorded as Brown" is not identified. Nothing shows which record, if either, matches how she identifies.
2. **Perception cannot be told from institutional practice or from copying.** The paper says so in one limitation sentence. The data allow the separation, and the key designs (movers, physician effects, death-hospital fixed effects, the infant's own hospital record) are all missing.
3. **"Black children" is not measured.** The unit is a child of a Black mother. The child's race depends on the father and on how the child looks, and neither is observed. Paper title, abstract and the "undercount of Black infants by two thirds" treat the mother's race as the child's.

Accept at a top journal only after the movers analysis (2.1), the certifier and copying analysis (2.2, 2.3) and the reference-validity analysis (2.4), and with claim rewording (section 5). The sibling papers use the same linkage, so the novelty claim must be sharpened (section 7).

## 1. Does the design answer "who records a Black person as Black"?

**What it identifies.**
- The marginal distribution of race in three records for linked pairs.
- Association between the hospital's record of the mother and the infant certificate within hospital (MH OR 2.04). This rules out a hospital-wide *constant* practice as the only driver.
- Net counts. These rule out the idea that the loss to Brown is merely symmetric noise on the hospital side.
- That unlinked and linked Black mothers' infants are certified alike (0.18 vs 0.19). This addresses selection into SUS-delivery, not into the linked-death sample.

**What it does not identify.**
- The identity of the person who classifies. Results name "hospital staff" and "certifier or informant" in Table 1, but the data say neither. Table 1's "Who classifies" column is an assumption, not a measurement. It also fails for the SINASC declaration, which the Introduction calls the mother's own.
- Perception of the person vs practice. The within-hospital OR is compatible with: (a) shared perception of the same mother and child; (b) *copying*: the certificate is written from the hospital's own chart, where the mother's or infant's race sits (75% of infants die where they were born, so the certifier has the same chart, and perhaps the same clerk, as the delivery admission); (c) shared within-hospital unit/ward/shift practice; (d) shared residual determinants (skin tone, schooling, neighbourhood, referral route) inside a hospital. The paper reads the OR as "which a hospital-wide practice cannot produce" and treats this as favouring perception. It favours (a), (b) and (c) equally. Copying is an institutional process and not perception.
- The third explanation, "infants are classified as lighter", is supported only by a cross-age contrast whose older-child rows come from a different estimator, population and set of municipalities. It also confounds age with who certifies (infant deaths in neonatal units vs older children at home or by SVO/IML), the cause of death and the informant.
- The delivery hospital's practice effect (1.20 per logit at equal composition) has an alternative: the same recording culture of the *region and staff pool* beyond the three-df spline on composition. A hospital-level coefficient of 1.2 has no causal reading as "practice".

## 2. Analyses the data allow but the paper lacks (ranked by effect on conclusions)

**2.1 Mother movers: person versus hospital effects (largest gain).**
- Source: SINASC.DN (RACACORMAE, CODESTAB, mother birth date, CODMUNRES, ESCMAE, IDADEMAE) linked within mother across births 2012-2023 (the project already links births to SIH deliveries; mother sibling linkage on mother birth date plus residence plus consistency checks is the same logic as the 1-4-year estimator), and `sinasc_births_to_delivery_admission` for the hospital record at each delivery.
- Specification A: among mothers declaring Black at their first birth (or at both), hospital-recorded Black at birth t as the outcome; mother fixed effects and delivery-hospital fixed effects (AKM on the connected set of hospitals joined by movers). Report the variance decomposition: person, hospital, residual, and the person-hospital match. If hospital effects carry most variance and the mother's effect little, institutional practice is shown. If the mother effect dominates, perception of the person is.
- Specification B: event study around a move to a hospital with a higher or lower practice share (the hospital's leave-one-out Black share): the hospital-recorded race of the same woman should shift by a fraction of the practice gap, and her SINASC declaration should not (or should, which then shows that SINASC is also hospital-driven).
- Specification C: concordance of the *same mother's SINASC declaration across births* (and across hospitals). The share who declare differently, and the direction (Black to Brown vs the reverse), measure how unstable the reference is. Report the instability by `TPFUNCRESP` and by hospital.

**2.2 Who certifies: crossed physician, establishment and municipality effects.**
- Source: SIM.DO `CRM` (certifying physician, from 2014, 0.97 filled), `ATESTANTE`, `CODESTAB`, `LOCOCOR`, `NECROPSIA`, `ASSISTMED`.
- Specification: logistic mixed model, outcome certified Black for infants of Black mothers, crossed random intercepts for CRM, death establishment, municipality; report the variance share of each and the ICC of physician after establishment. Then add the hospital's record of the mother as a fixed term: does it survive physician and establishment effects? Compare physicians certifying both Black- and Brown-mother infants (physician FE contrast), which removes physician identity from the comparison.
- This shows whether the "institution" claim holds up, or whether the category depends on the individual doctor.

**2.3 Copy channel versus perception: death-hospital fixed effects and the infant's own record.**
- Source: SIM.DO CODESTAB, LOCOCOR; SIH.RD for the infant's admission (RACA_COR of the infant at age 0 for infants who died in hospital; the project already links SIH to SIM for adult women, `sih_to_sim`); SIH.RD and SINASC `RACACOR` / `RACACOR_RN` (2011-2013 newborn field) for the newborn's colour.
- Specification A: restrict to the 25% who died in a hospital other than the delivery hospital. With death-establishment fixed effects, does the delivery hospital's record of the mother (a chart the certifier could not read) still predict the certificate? A near-zero coefficient there with a strong one among same-hospital deaths indicates copying. A persisting coefficient indicates a shared property of the person.
- Specification B: infant's own SIH-recorded race against the certificate for in-hospital deaths. If concordance is near 1, the certificate copies the chart and the certifier has no classification role. If low, the certifier decides independently.
- Specification C: deaths at home or in the street (no chart) against hospital deaths, by `LOCOCOR`.
- Specification D: SIM `TPPOS` / `FONTEINV` / `FONTES` (interview source) among investigated infant deaths: is the category used more where an interview with the family was done (the Ministry's intended procedure)? This is the nearest the data come to "informant asked".

**2.4 Validity of the reference: who filled the birth record, and the newborn's colour.**
- Source: SINASC `TPFUNCRESP` (doctor, nurse, midwife, registry-office clerk, other; 0.96 filled), `RACACOR` newborn (0.94 filled, 2011-2013 variants), `ORIGEM`, `DTDECLARAC`.
- Specification: mother's SINASC-declared Black share and the SINASC-versus-SIH agreement by `TPFUNCRESP`. Registry-office clerks ask the mother in a different setting from nurses at the bed. If the SINASC-declared Black share and the agreement with SIH differ by filler, the "declaration" is partly the filler's.
- Mother-versus-newborn colour in SINASC: the manual records that they match in about half the pairs. The paper never uses it. It bears on "Black children" (the infant's own colour recorded by the same staff) and gives the share of Black mothers' newborns recorded Black at birth, which can then be compared with the certificate at death for the same infant (same child, two staff, a few weeks apart).

**2.5 The hospital's practice, built and tested properly (replace the current facility model).**
- Source: SIH delivery link (13.45 million pairs), CNES.ST (ownership, nature, teaching, SUS), CNES.HB (beds), CNES.PF/EP (professional categories, headcount of obstetricians and nurses per delivery), CNES.LT.
- Specification: leave-one-out practice (excluding the infant's own mother, and mothers of the deaths), empirical-Bayes shrunk with hospital random effect (the 30-mother floor makes raw shares noisy and the logit(+0.01) transform arbitrary), adjusted for case mix of Black mothers in each hospital (schooling, age, region, shade of area), with errors clustered by *hospital*, not municipality. Add ownership and teaching status as moderators: is practice larger in teaching or private-contracted hospitals? Also practice at the *municipality's other hospitals* to separate hospital from local culture.
- A measurement-error correction (errors-in-variables) for the attenuated coefficient: it is probably understated.

**2.6 Time: administrative explanations for the rise in the certificate.**
- Source: SINASC `TPFUNCRESP` and `ORIGEM` by year; SIM `ATESTANTE`; the 2012 change in birth-record coding; the linked share of deaths (0.77 to 0.82); state-level training or policy dates (Portaria on race recording; public-health campaigns: not in the lake, so named as limitation).
- Specification: decompose the certificate's rise into composition (changing mix of mothers declaring Black, 5.6% to 7.9%) and within-composition change. The growth in the share of mothers declaring Black means the *population* of Black mothers changed, so a rising certificate share can be selection of who declares, not recording behaviour. Show the rise for mothers who were already Black in earlier births.

**2.7 SIH admissions of the same women at other times.**
- Source: SIH.RD (all admissions, including non-delivery: abortion, prenatal complications, postpartum, other causes), linked by the delivery link's person key where available.
- Specification: the same woman's recorded race across admissions to different hospitals and years (stability of the hospital record within person), as a test of whether hospital race fields track the person at all. Extend the adult-women supplement (28% false pairs) with a cleaner source: women with two or more linked admissions.

**2.8 Blank hospital race.** The 21.8% blank is a recording choice and may be informative (blanks differ by declared race: 0.15 White, 0.22 Black, 0.24 Brown). Model blank as an outcome by hospital; show blank-by-hospital against practice; report results under bounds (all blanks Black, all blanks Brown).

**2.9 Heterogeneity the paper has the data for.** By mother's schooling, age, region, prenatal care, delivery type; by infant cause of death and neonatal versus post-neonatal period; by day of week and shift of death (`DTOBITO`, `HORAOBITO` if held); by ICU admission. Currently only the multilevel model reports some, with no interpretation.

## 4. Statistical issues

1. **Facility model clustering.** Practice is a hospital-level covariate, yet errors are clustered by *municipality* (`black.py`, `mun6` groups). Cluster by delivery hospital (or two-way). Intervals for 1.20 (1.14; 1.26) are probably too narrow, especially with 1,040 hospitals and hospitals sharing municipalities unequally.
2. **Circularity and attenuation.** Practice (2012-2023) includes the infants' own mothers. Use leave-one-out. The practice measure (share over 30+ mothers) has sampling error; the coefficient is attenuated, and `+0.01` in the logit is arbitrary (influences hospitals with shares near 0 or 1, i.e., the p10 = 0.01 hospitals). Use a hierarchical estimate.
3. **Within-hospital MH OR (443 hospitals).** MH uses only hospitals with variation in both strata, discarding 600 of 1,040. Report how many deaths contribute, the heterogeneity (Breslow-Day or a random-effects OR), and an adjusted conditional logistic model (mother's schooling, age, birth weight, region/year). The OR 2.04 is conditional on declaration Black; a model on all mothers with hospital FE and an interaction would use more data.
4. **Non-monotone gradient.** Infants of mothers recorded White by the hospital are certified Black more (0.17) than those recorded Brown (0.12), among Black-declared mothers. This is reported and not interpreted; a pure perception or copying model predicts monotonicity. It suggests heterogeneity in recording noise or a residual group of hospitals recording White as default (southern hospitals). Needs a cross-tab by region.
5. **Net count uncertainty.** The net ratios 0.74 and 0.34 have no confidence intervals (bootstrap over hospitals / municipalities). The 0.34 uses numerator counts of all linked deaths certified Black (7,560) against Black mothers' infants (22,305): this is defined on the declaration being truth (a Brown-declared mother's Black-certified infant counts as an "error" that offsets). A ratio of 0.34 is not "the infant certificate undercounts Black infants by about two thirds": it is the ratio of persons certified Black to infants of Black-declaring mothers (see 5.2).
6. **Multiple testing and forks.** Two primaries are named; the abstract nevertheless reports about 20 estimates. Say which results are confirmatory and give the denominators plainly.
7. **Estimator for children 1-9.** The 1-4 and 5-9 estimates use a differencing estimator; the 5-9 interval (0.23; 0.36) overlaps the infant and the 1-4 intervals, the supplement states that estimates can fall outside 0-1 and that the denominator variance is ignored. The text claims a gradient with age: only the 1-4 vs infant contrast (0.24 vs 0.18) is arguably supported, and only if the variance ignoring the denominator is corrected (delta method or bootstrap on both).
8. **Adult women.** 28.2% false pairs removed in expectation, n=5,579, hospital deaths in small municipalities. Intervals (0.48; 0.63) rest on assumptions (false pairs follow regional composition). A row in the main Table 1 and the Abstract gives it more weight than "exploratory" allows. Move to supplement only.
9. **Multilevel models.** nAGQ = 0 gives biased variance components for binary outcomes with small clusters (2,766 municipalities, 22,289 deaths: about 8 per cluster). Re-fit with nAGQ = 7+ or Laplace or Bayesian; show the sensitivity. "Variance explained 58%" and the sequence in S4 are order-dependent (non-additive; one row has -1.4%); the percent-of-variance numbers are not decompositions. The 0.31 between-establishment vs 0.10 between-municipality variance is the more informative result and it is a single sentence.
10. **Moran's I and BYM2.** The map is "smoothed strongly" with few deaths per municipality; it carries little information beyond region. Consider dropping or moving it to the supplement; do not interpret "residual clustering 0.13" without a null from the same model.
11. **Time model.** Slopes for regions "without a clear difference" while the text reports 0.19 to 0.99; fine, but the national slope 0.42 (0.20; 0.64) is from a mixed model on intermediate regions, while hospital trend +0.11 (0.05; 0.17) is a quasi-binomial on counts. They are different estimators on different scales and populations (the hospital series is not a fixed cohort; records with hospital link double over time). "Faster on the certificate than in the hospital" needs a joint model (same population, same year definition, difference with CI), not two separate CIs. The hospital rate (birth-year) and the certificate rate (death-year) mix cohorts.
12. **Hospital series 2023.** 119,012 mothers in 2023 against about 90,000: the 2023 linkage may be incomplete or composition changed; the drop to 0.36 in 2023 after 0.38 in 2022 should be checked before "rose".
13. **Unit mix in Table 1.** Rows differ in population, year, estimator and denominator (blanks excluded in hospital, included in certificate). The note admits it. A ladder with different denominators invites exactly the misreading it warns of. Put every row on the same denominator (blank counted in both, or excluded in both).

## 5. Claims stronger than the evidence

1. Title and Abstract: "Black children recorded as Brown". *Why:* the unit is the infant of a Black-declaring mother (SINASC), and the child's own race is unobserved. *Reword:* "Infants of Black mothers recorded as Brown on death certificates" (and drop "Black children" in the conclusion).
2. Abstract: "the infant certificate undercounts Black infants by about two thirds even net." *Why:* an undercount of "Black infants" requires a gold standard for the infant's race. The comparison is certified-Black infants against infants of Black-declaring mothers (a count ratio, 0.34). *Reword:* "certificates recorded 0.34 as many infants as Black as there were infants of mothers who declared themselves Black."
3. Abstract and Discussion: "The certificate follows the hospital's record of the same mother." *Why:* an association (OR 2.04, 0.28 vs 0.12) is a direction, not a mechanism; 72% of infants of Black mothers recorded Black by the hospital were still not certified Black. *Reword:* "was associated with"; give the 0.28 explicitly, not 'follows'.
4. Discussion: "which a hospital-wide practice cannot produce." *Why:* copying from a shared chart, ward-level practice and shared residual determinants all produce it (section 1). *Reword:* "which a constant hospital-wide practice alone cannot produce; copying from the chart, ward practice, or shared perception can."
5. Discussion: "hospital staff and certifiers perceive the same mother and child, so that appearance leads both to the Black category; it fits..." *Why:* appearance is unobserved; a theory fitting the data is not tested against the rival (copying). *Reword:* list it as one of three untested readings and state the tests (2.1, 2.3).
6. Abstract: "the delivery hospital's practice predicted the certificate at equal composition, region and year." *Why:* the practice is estimated on the same mothers, attenuated and clustered at the wrong level (section 4.1, 4.2). *Reword:* "was associated with, with OR 1.20 (1.14; 1.26) per logit unit, at equal composition...", and add that the measure includes the infants' mothers.
7. Abstract and Conclusion: "Black families are recorded as Brown more often than as Black in Brazil's health records." *Why:* 0.53 among those with a race on the hospital record, 0.41 of all admissions; for the census (0.41 Brown vs 0.39 Black, 2010) families do the same; "more often than as Black" is not true for the census row; and the certificate's Brown 0.55 is among infants of mothers recorded by staff. The sentence also ignores that SINASC "declared Black" is the denominator. *Reword:* "Black mothers are recorded as Black in a minority of hospital records and certificates."
8. Abstract: "so statistics of the Black population built from these records undercount it unevenly across the country." *Why:* "unevenly" rests on hospital practice variance (p10 0.01, p90 0.64: not shown to be recording and not case mix) and on municipal variance that mixes noise and composition; the country map is unreliable (few deaths per municipality). *Reword:* "undercount it, by amounts that differ between hospitals."
9. Discussion: "the category was used more for older children than for infants" and Conclusion "least for infants": the 1-4 and 5-9 estimates rely on a different estimator in a restricted sample, and the 5-9 interval overlaps. *Reword:* "at 1-4 years in the same municipalities, 0.24 against 0.18; the 5-9 estimate is imprecise".
10. Discussion: "A certificate completed from the record at hand rather than by asking the informant ... would carry both the hospital's perception and its practice." *Why:* speculative and untested (2.3 can test it). *Reword:* test, or label it a hypothesis.
11. Discussion on the rise: "faster on the certificate than in the hospital, and coincided with more mothers declaring themselves Black". *Why:* the compositional change in who declares (5.6% to 7.9%) is an alternative explanation of the rise; the hospital share is not a fixed cohort. *Reword:* decompose first (2.6).
12. Implications: "auditing hospitals' records against their patients' declarations, which linked records already allow." *Why:* the declaration is itself a staff record. *Reword:* "auditing hospitals' records against the birth record, noting that the latter is also entered by staff."
13. Introduction: "We know of no study..." fine, but "the verification commissions ... these records show how far observation and declaration diverge at the Black boundary": the records do not show observation (skin colour) at all. Delete.

## 6. Presentation and what to cut

- **Cut or move to supplement:** BYM2 map (Figure 4A) and Moran's I; state-versus-composition deviance (37% vs 22%), which belongs to paper 1; Table S4's ten-row sequence (keep three rows); the adult-women row of Table 1, Figure 1 and the abstract; the 5-9-year row; the census ladder as a headline row; the regional slope range.
- **Merge:** Results "Who certifies" and the multilevel model into one paragraph with the three numbers that matter (establishment variance, SVO/IML, mother's age).
- **Add:** a single figure of the three-record ladder on one denominator with net counts as a second panel; a diagram of the linkage (counts at each step: 309,203 deaths, 291,812 linked, 22,305 Black-mother infants, 10,568 with hospital record); a table of who is in the 10,568 (region, schooling, hospital type) against the 22,305.
- **Abstract:** 14 estimates; keep six (three-record ladder, net counts, within-hospital OR, practice OR, certifier effect, trend).
- **Discussion** repeats the Results; its three explanations should become a table of the three readings with their predictions and the tests (2.1-2.4).
- **Methods** are dense; the Analysis paragraph mixes 8 models. Put models in a supplement table.
- **Terminology:** "the hospital's record of the mother" vs "practice" vs "classification": define once. "Declared" for SINASC is the paper's key claim: footnote what the DNV form says and who fills it (TPFUNCRESP).
- **Ethics and data:** state the direct linkage's false-link estimate per step in the main text, not only in Methods parentheses.

## 7. Overlap with sibling papers

- **Paper 1 (place)** carries the same linked infant file and the *same Black-mother number* (0.18 (0.17; 0.20), rise 0.15 to 0.21 in 2013-2023), the same BYM2 map, Moran's I, composition-versus-state deviance and municipal multilevel variances (composition explained 87% of municipal variance there, 39% here: different outcome subsets, but the contrast is not discussed). Paper 3's "Where and when" section duplicates paper 1; cut it to two sentences and cite. The 0.15 to 0.21 trend appears in both abstracts: say which paper owns it.
- **Paper 2 (indicators)** reports the Black-to-Brown rate ratio 1.15 by the mother's race against 0.49 by certified race, which is the *consequence* of the 0.18 number in this paper. Cite it as the downstream effect rather than re-deriving the undercount. Neither paper mentions the other's claim that the certificate is "a third classification" (paper 1) while paper 3 says the certificate "follows the hospital's record": reconcile (is the certificate independent of the hospital, a third classification, or a copy?). That is exactly the question 2.3 answers.
- **Contradiction risk:** paper 1 says place (composition) explains 71% of between-municipality deviance for Brown mothers' certified-White share, and paper 3 reports 39% of Black-category variance by composition and a stronger establishment effect. The establishment variance (0.31) here is larger than the municipal (0.10); paper 1's narrative is municipal. Paper 3's finding changes paper 1's interpretation and should be stated in both.
- **Shared limitation** (mother's race as reference, child's race unobserved) must be stated identically in all three.
- Each paper must state its own contribution in the introduction: paper 3 owns the hospital-record link and the institution-versus-person question; once 2.1-2.4 are added, that is a stand-alone paper.
