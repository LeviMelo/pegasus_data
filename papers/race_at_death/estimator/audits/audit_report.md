# Independent methods audit: the estimator paper (paper 6)

Scope: code (`modelled_race.py` 1063-1290, `analysis/estimator.py`, `analysis/simulate.py`), text (`estimator/text*.py`), JSON, built docx. Read-only.

## Major

1. **"Measurable" bias contradicts the paper's own finding (Abstract Conclusions vs Discussion "Assumptions").** The abstract says moving is the bias, "measurable and small". The Discussion says the data "cannot identify it without linked records". The only empirical measure of the non-found, the admissions (10% of linked units; certified White recorded White 50.2% vs 62.8%), is a 13-point gap, not small. The simulation's movers are small by construction (they differ only through a smooth decile gradient of destination). Fix: replace "measurable and small" with "unidentified without linked records; 13 points in the admissions", and say the simulation's smallness is an input.

2. **Coverage claims come from the "ideal" scenario but read as general.** `text.py:33,96`, SIM_FINDING (`text_results.py:86`), abstract: "unbiased under twins, movers... its model intervals covered 92-98%". Those figures are `ideal` only (`cover_range("ideal",...)`). simulation.json: model coverage of the system's value is 82-98.5% (movers), 79.5-98.5% (unequal), 0-98.5% (selective); the Results text never says so. Fix: report the range per scenario and state that coverage against all deaths fails where selection operates.

3. **"Unbiased" and "selection, not the estimator" do not survive the numbers.** Ideal, 6,400-25,599, Black-as-Black: bias +0.0093, MCSE 0.0032 (2.9 MCSE). The abstract's "unbiased ... largest bias 0.014" sits beside "movers biased it ... by up to 0.013": the estimator's own worst bias exceeds the selection bias it is contrasted with. At 6,400-25,599 the movers' bias (+0.0125) is almost wholly the estimator's (bias_found +0.0112). The `assert small_bf<0.01 and big_bf<0.02` is not a test of unbiasedness. Fix: report |bias|/MCSE, call the residual small-row ratio-estimator bias, and limit the selection claim to size classes where it holds (<400).

4. **MCSE of the bias is wrong (`simulate.py:187`).** `mcse_bias = sd(est)/sqrt(n)`; bias is mean(est - truth) with a replicate-specific truth, so the MCSE needs sd(est - truth) (Morris 2019, the cited ADEMP source). Figure 3 bars inherit it. Fix: use the sd of est - truth.

5. **The chance interval is called "the right yardstick for validation" but fails in both validations (Discussion "Variance").** `outside_chance` against findable pairs: 3, 6, 6, 2, 4 of 20 per size class (all: 7 of 20; nominal 1); infants 4, 5, 2, 0 of 20. Table 1 and Results report only model-SE exceedances (9 of 80 = 11%, itself above 5%). In simulation the chance coverage is 90-99%, so the real data carry error the simulation lacks. Candidate causes in the code: (a) units sharing a key cell (`d.n>1`): `V` uses d.n*b.n, the variance is d.n^2*b.n; (b) the same birth records are chance candidates for one sex's deaths and controls for the other's (covariance ignored); (c) a scaled control has variance s*mu, not mu (`v = 2*control`), and s reaches ~4 in a deaths register (15-29 years); (d) zero-width intervals where control = 0; (e) ~1% false links in the truth. Fix: report outside_chance in Tables 1-2; correct the variance for (a)-(c) or retract "right yardstick"; add a simulation row with realistic same-key clustering.

6. **Infant validation compares different populations (`estimator.py:91`, Methods Validation 1).** Truth = the 80.5% of deaths that linked (needs birth weight, mother's age); the estimator covers all units (found 87-93%, more than linked). The admissions analysis shows such selection moves cells by 0.006 (0.010-0.055 in some classes), the same order as the infant "errors" (0.0045-0.020). No findable/unfindable split for infants. Fix: restrict the truth to linked pairs that share the key and report both.

7. **No naive baseline; the admissions validation barely exercises the control.** Chance per unit is 0.008-0.09 in four of five size classes (Table 2), and "findable" is defined with the estimator's own key, so agreement of 0.0003-0.0013 is close to tautological (a unique same-key certificate is the partner). Missing comparators: candidates without control, and unique-candidate links. Without them the paper cannot show the subtraction matters. Fix: add both to Tables 1-2 (infants, with large chance counts, is where it should show).

8. **The simulation cannot answer two stated aims.** (a) The cluster SE, the headline of the variance section, is not assessed in the text; municipalities are fixed across replicates and between-municipality variation is only the decile gradient, so cluster SE ~ model SE ~ empirical sd in `ideal` (0.0149/0.0147/0.0149, <400, Black-as-Black) and nothing discriminates. The real-data gap (cluster/counts 9.2 vs model/counts 0.89, admissions) and the explanation "classification varies between cities" are untested. (b) A2 holds by construction (constant 0.512 male share, class-independent), yet the Discussion names A2 as the threat in a deaths register. All deaths (77,693) fall in one cohort, about 12 times the real per-cohort density (`simulate.py:67`), inflating same-day death density and shared-candidate dependence (finding 5). Fix: add an A2-violating scenario, a realistic-density scenario, and cluster coverage, or drop the claims.

## Minor

9. **"Found" truth misclassifies movers who land in their origin (`simulate.py:88,128`).** Destinations are drawn in proportion to births, so a mover can land in its own municipality (frequent for large cells), is then found but excluded from `found`; this inflates `bias_found` in large classes (finding 3). Fix: exclude the origin or define found by `r == m`.

10. **"Found" conflates two things.** `arrays()` drops declared class "other" (Asian/blank) from T but keeps those deaths in D, so `ideal` found is 95.9%, not ~100%, and "twins raise found" mixes artifacts. Admissions: found 83.2% vs findable 86.2%; the gap is attributed to the four-race restriction but not shown. Fix: define D over the same classes and compare like with like.

11. **Variance code vs documentation.** The docstring says `ratio_se_poisson` is the counts-only practice, but it is the delta method with denominators (the model SE for ratios), so for ratios "counts" and "model" coincide; it is non-zero for the reference class (ratio = 1) while `ratio_se_chance` zeroes it. Supplement S1 says unstable rows are "reported as not estimable": no such rule in code (a 0.15 SE cut-off appears only in Figure 2; rows under 30 pairs are dropped from the truth). Fix: unify and state the rule.

12. **Derivation wording (Methods "The estimator").** Row proportions are said to need "A1 alone"; they also need A2 (and A3 for twins). "Three assumptions make it unbiased" but E[T] = tN plus twins; the row sums estimate the declared mix only if t is constant across j (said later, for ratios only). Wald intervals are not bounded to [0,1]; the ratio of sums is biased in small rows (Supplement admits it, Methods does not).

13. **The 25,600 cut-off** is chosen from the same data (mean difference rises smoothly 0.0045 to 0.020 before it); "all" rows in Tables 1-2 include the failing class; Table 1 municipality counts do not sum (size is by municipality-year). The failing class is the largest cities, which conflicts with the Discussion advice for "a national table". Fix: say it is post hoc, show "all" without it, and give the share of births excluded.

14. **Validation does not test the application.** The estimator is used at ages 1-14 where movers matter (found ~72%); infants (found ~90%) and admissions (key errors, not moving) probe other mechanisms. Make this the first limitation.

15. **Writing.** The Introduction says the estimator "was the only source of the mother's race for each death": it gives tables, not a per-death source. Results say "Black mothers" while Methods say "race on the birth record" (child's or mother's declaration). Cluster unit (municipality pooled over years) is not stated.

## Note

16. **References (reading_notes.md: abstracts only, full texts unread).** lipsitch2010 supports the term "negative control" (detecting confounding), not quantitative subtraction; word it as borrowing the idea. lash2014 is cited for the t_a/t_b scaling, which is algebra, not a result there. tancredi2011 is cited as sharing "propagation of the error from the matching to the estimate"; this estimator propagates no matching uncertainty. pita2018 detail ("names and addresses") rests on the abstract. Missing: false-match-rate and linkage-free contingency-table literature (e.g. Belin and Rubin 1995; Chipperfield et al. 2011, dropped only because Crossref matched another work, so retrieve it) and the linearisation basis (Binder 1983, in notes, uncited).

## Checks that passed
Spot-checked against the JSON, all matched: 383,975 / 309,203 = 80.5%; false links 0.57-0.75% and 0.66-1.00%; 3,766,913 units; linked 95.9%, findable 86.2% (10.1% non-findable of linked); 50.2% vs 62.8%; found 83.2%; infant mean |diff| 0.0045-0.0202, max 0.096, 9 of 80; chance per unit 0.0079 and 0.908; ideal found 95.9%, twins 97.0%; t-ratio 0.78/0.70 = 1.11; 28 of 200 invalid. Delta-method formulas for p, mix, found and ratio, the 2x control for the chance variance, and the cluster linearisation (M/(M-1) sum z^2) are algebraically right for the stated independence model. The problems are interpretive (1-3, 5) and design (6-8), not transcription.

## Verdict
Sound idea, correct algebra for its stated model, but the manuscript overstates the evidence: coverage and unbiasedness are quoted from the benign scenario, the chance interval fails in the real validations, the infant truth is a selected subset, and the "measurable" conclusion is contradicted by the admissions result and the Discussion. Revise findings 1-8 before calling the estimator validated.
