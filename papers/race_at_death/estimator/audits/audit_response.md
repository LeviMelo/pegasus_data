| 1 | "measurable and small" contradicts the Discussion | Accepted. The Conclusions now say the bias (the selection of who kept the key) is not measurable from public files, and give the one place it was measured: the admissions, a gap of 13 points in one cell. The Discussion says the simulation's smallness under movers is an input (only the decile gradient differs between places) and that it shows the mechanism, not its size. |
| 2 | coverage quoted from `ideal` only | Accepted. The abstract gives the lowest (selective). |
| 3 | "unbiased" does not survive; the estimator's own bias exceeds the selection | Accepted. The text now says "nearly unbiased": at most the small-class value below 6,400 births a year, and a small-row ratio bias at 6,400-25,599 given in Monte Carlo errors. The selection claim is made where it holds (<400: movers +0.013 against all deaths, -0.0003 against the found). The rerun with `sparse` showed that "nearly unbiased" holds only with the real number of deaths (ideal, twins: 0.004 below 6,400 births, 0.009 above); with a twelfth of them the ratio-of-sums bias reaches 0.020 and 0.134. The text and its assert now say so, rather than widening the bound. |

The sentence "right yardstick" was removed; Table 2 now reports the cells outside both intervals. The covariance between the two sexes' deaths sharing births (b) is second order and not modelled; said in S1 by omission, not claimed. |
