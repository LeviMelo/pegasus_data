Supplementary material - Cross-classifying two registers without linking them: candidates less a negative control from birth date, sex and municipality, tested on Brazilian birth, death and hospital records
Supplementary Text S1 - The estimator's expectation and its four variances
Write F(j, k) for the units of G found among their candidates (the person kept the key) and B(j, k) = sum over units certified k of the chance candidates of class j. Then the candidates summed are F(j, k) + B(j, k) + W(j, k), W the related records sharing the key (same-sex twins), and the control summed is K(j, k) = B'(j, k) + W'(j, k), B' the other sex's chance records scaled by s and W' the other-sex twins. Under A2, E[B'] = E[B]; under A3, W and W' fall in the person's own cell. So E[T(j, k)] = E[F(j, k)] + E[W - W'], and under A1 E[F(j, k)] = t_j N(j, k). The row proportion is a ratio of such sums: consistent as the units of a row grow, biased in small rows, and unstable when a row sum is near zero (a class rare among the units and common among the chance candidates). Intervals are Wald intervals and are not bounded to 0-1. In the text and tables no rule withholds an estimate; the figures do not draw estimates whose standard error is 0.15 or more, and rows of the known answers with fewer than 30 pairs are left out of the comparisons.
Units that share a key cell share their candidates and their control whatever their class; the package's variance columns group by key, class, age, cause and year, the analysis here by key and class (the key cell's deaths of a class, g, enter as g squared). Let c be the records of a key cell of the unit's sex and o those of the other sex, s the scale. (i) Counts: Var T taken as the candidates plus the control summed, and a proportion's standard error sqrt(Var T(j, k)) / T(j, +), as if the row sum were known. (ii) Model: with the register's records Poisson, Var T(j, k) = sum of g^2 (c + s^2 o) over the cells; with independent cells the proportion p = T(j, k) / T(j, +) has Var p = [(1 - p)^2 V(j, k) + p^2 (V(j, +) - V(j, k))] / T(j, +)^2; the declared mix is the same with rows in place of cells, and a ratio of rates by declared class a over b has Var log r = V(a, +) / T(a, +)^2 + V(b, +) / T(b, +)^2, the person-years being complete counts of births. (iii) Exact model: in a key cell write x for the boys' deaths by class less the girls' over beta (beta the ratio of boys to girls in the municipality and birth year) and y for the girls' less beta times the boys'; the cell's T(j, k) is c_boys(j) x_k + c_girls(j) y_k, so for the linear form a'T(j, .) with a = e_k - p_jk the variance is a' M_j a with M_j the sum over cells of x x' c_boys(j) + y y' c_girls(j), which carries the covariances between classes of deaths sharing a cell and between the sexes; divided by T(j, +)^2 it is Var p_jk. With one death a cell it equals (ii). (iv) Chance: for the table of these units only the chance candidates (expected s o) and the control vary, V = sum of g^2 o (s + s^2), with the same delta method. (v) Clusters: with z_m the linearised contribution of municipality m (for p: [T_m(j, k) - p T_m(j, +)] / T(j, +)), Var = M / (M - 1) sum z_m^2 over the M municipalities of the group, years pooled. The rate ratios of the companion papers at 1-9 years were counted before the g and s terms were added; there V was taken with g = s = 1.
(ii) and (iii) answer the question asked of a classification as a property of the system that produced the deaths; (iv) the question of how far the estimate is from the table that a perfect linkage of the same units would give; (v) the first question without the Poisson model, treating the municipalities as a sample from a population of municipalities, so that variation of the classification between them enters the error.
Supplementary Text S2 - The simulation
Births. Each municipality's 2018 births by the race on the birth record (White, Black, Brown, Indigenous, and Asian or blank) were spread over the 365 days with weights for the weekday (Monday to Sunday 1.06, 1.08, 1.07, 1.06, 1.04, 0.88, 0.81, departures from one multiplied by 1.5 for White and 0.8 for Indigenous births) and a seasonal cosine of amplitude 0.05, and drawn as Poisson counts by day, sex (51.2% boys; in scenario a2 52.5% of White and 50.5% of Black and Indigenous births) and race. Twins (scenarios with twins): pairs drawn at 1% of births by day and race, two thirds of the same sex. Deaths: each birth died with a probability proportional to 0.66, 1.1, 1.0, 4.1 and 1.0 for the five classes, twins at twice it, scaled to the deaths of 2013-2024 at 1-9 years (scenario sparse: a twelfth of them). Movers: a death moved with the scenario's probability to a municipality of the same state chosen in proportion to its births (a draw of its own municipality being no move), and was resident there at death. Certified race: drawn from the infant linkage's proportions for the decile of the White share of births of the municipality of residence at death (rows with fewer than 50 linked pairs: the national proportions), a child declared Asian or blank as a Brown one; in the selective scenario the race is drawn where the child was born and the mover keeps it.
The estimator was computed as for the real data: candidates and the other sex's births of the death's day in its municipality of residence, scaled by the municipality's ratio of boys to girls, deaths sharing a key and a class counted together. Every replicate drew a new world; 200 replicates a scenario (100 for the two pooled ones). Monte Carlo error of the bias: the standard deviation of estimate less truth over the replicates, over the square root of their number. Targets: the table of all deaths of the size class (the estimand), of those found among their candidates (for the bias of the estimator itself), and the mean of the first over replicates (the system's classification, for the coverage of the model and cluster errors).
