# Review 2 - paper 2, `indicators/` (reviewer report)

Read: `out/manuscript.txt`, `out/supplement.txt`, `text*.py`, audit 1 and its response, `results.json` (infant_rates, children, census, decomposition, validation), `spatial.json` (ratio, misstated), `analysis/results.py`, `analysis/spatial.py`, `analysis/models.R` (stage ratio), Figures 1 and 2, the three sibling manuscripts, `DATA_SOURCES.md` (SINASC colour fields). Nothing run except read-only Python on the JSON and simple arithmetic on the flow matrix (marked "my arithmetic"; verify before use).

## 0. Overall judgement: major revision

The central observation is real and large: with deaths and births both classified by the mother, White to Brown is 0.81 and Black to Brown 1.15; with certified deaths over the mother's births it is 1.31 and 0.49. The arithmetic of audit 1 holds. But as a journal paper the contribution is a demonstration of a known numerator-denominator inconsistency (Hahn 1992, Arias 2016, Caldas 2017 are in the reference list), and the design does not yet separate three things the abstract runs together: (a) certifier misclassification, (b) the legitimate difference between the mother's race and the child's race, and (c) the denominator. The data in the paper's own flow matrix show that (b) and (c), not (a), carry most of the White to Brown "reversal". The national ratios are crude (no region, year, maternal covariates), the certified-ratio CI is wrong in its construction, the regional layer is largely prior-driven, and the data allow much more (age at death, cause, time, counterfactual, the Indigenous group, Ministry comparator) that a demanding referee will ask for. Publishable as a short report after changes in section 8 below; the headline must be reframed.

## 1. Errors

**1.1 The certified-ratio intervals are Poisson only; the Methods say otherwise.** Methods: "National ratios carry the larger of the Poisson standard error and a delete-one-state jackknife". In `results.py` the jackknife is applied to `ratio_mother` only (line 69-80; the infant candidate ratio at 476-478; the linked ratio at 458 uses the jackknife alone). `ratio_certificate` has `est` and `se` only: 1.31 (1.30; 1.32) and 0.49 (0.48; 0.50) are Poisson counts. State certified ratios run from 0.95 (SC) to 4.13 (AL) (Table S2, 1-9 y), so a state jackknife would give an interval an order of magnitude wider. The ratios of ratios (reversal) therefore have no valid interval. Fix the sentence and recompute (jackknife or bootstrap over municipalities or regions; better a model with region effects, see 5.1).

**1.2 The infant mother's-race rates treat scaled counts as Poisson counts, and the scaling is wrong for the Indigenous group.** Rates are `linked deaths x 1.295` (`linkage_scale = 383,975/296,512`, `results.py` 463-466): one factor for all races. The linked share by certified race is 81.1/81.1/80.2 for White/Brown/Black but 61.9 for Indigenous (stated in Methods). Unlinked deaths have a known certified race, so the scale should be by certified race (and region and year), not global. For White/Brown/Black this changes little; for Indigenous it matters. My arithmetic: certified Indigenous deaths implied by Table 1 (26.6 x 0.280M births) is about 7.4 thousand; the flow matrix applied to the scaled mother's-race deaths gives about 5.9 thousand certified Indigenous deaths, a 20% gap that is exactly the unlinked deficit. With a 1/0.62 scale for the deaths certified Indigenous, the Indigenous mother's rate is about 27, not 21.7, and the Indigenous to Brown ratio about 2.0, not 1.60 (1.46; 1.75). Also, 4.1% of linked deaths have no mother's race or a Yellow mother (309,203 vs 296,512) and are folded into the scale: say so. In the regional models the scaled counts are rounded and entered as Poisson `y` (`models.R`, `round(A$mothDW)`), which overstates precision by about the scale (sqrt 1.3 on the SE) and is not a count at all.

**1.3 "Brown children's excess mortality" (Conclusion, Abstract) is wrong as written.** The ratio by the mother's race is a ratio for children of Brown, White, Black mothers. The child's own race is a different quantity (see 2.1). Replace "Brown children" by "children of Brown mothers" or demonstrate the claim for children's own classification.

**1.4 Regional "credible reversal" ignores the dependence between the two models.** `misstated_sd = hypot(certificate_sd, mother_sd)` (`spatial.py` 403) assumes independent posteriors; the two models use the same births and the same deaths (the mother's-race deaths are the certified deaths reallocated), so the covariance is positive and unknown. The counts 469 (credible ratio of ratios), 115 (joint) rest on this. Compute the ratio of ratios from one joint model (see 5.3) or by posterior sampling of a flow model.

**1.5 The national model term and the national crude ratio disagree and the text does not reconcile them.** Model fixed terms: mother's exp(-0.107) = 0.90 (crude 0.81), certified exp(0.757) = 2.13 (crude 1.31). A gap of 63% for certified is not shrinkage; it is Simpson-type composition: White births concentrate in South/Southeast where all mortality is lower, Brown in North/Northeast, so the pooled White rate is dragged down. The within-region (adjusted) ratios are therefore further from 1 than the crude national ones in both classifications. The crude national "0.81" and "1.31" are thus confounded by geography; no region-standardised national ratio (Mantel-Haenszel or fixed-effects over regions) is given. This is both an error of interpretation (the paper uses 1.31 as "the" ratio of the usual indicator) and a missing analysis (3.2).

**1.6 Minor.** "By posterior means ... 400 of 510" should state shrinkage to 0.90 explicitly in the Abstract. The text "0.66 ... in the same years, all municipalities" for 2016-2024 is right but nearly identical to the 2013-2024 value (0.663 vs 0.663 in `results.json`); print the period in the table note. Table 1 labels White/Black/Indigenous rows with "per 100,000" without "person-years" in the shown figure panels B and C.

## 2. Does the design answer the question?

**2.1 What "reversal" means.** The paper takes the mother's declaration at the birth record as the reference and calls a departure from it a reversal. But the certificate records the *child's* race (as an informant or staff classify the child), the birth record the *mother's* race (SINASC `RACACOR` is the mother's race copied; the newborn's own `RACACORN` is filed on a few hundred records, `DATA_SOURCES.md`). A child of a Brown mother and a White father may be correctly White, and the census households classify 27.7% of Brown mothers' newborns as White, 11.0% of White mothers' newborns as Brown, 19.1% of Black mothers' newborns as White and 40.6% as Brown (2010 Census, age 0, `results.json` decomposition/census). The certificate's flows at under one year are 25.2%, 17.4%, 18.2% and 54.8%. So for the White-Brown boundary the certificate moves Brown mothers' infants to White at 91% of the household rate (0.252 vs 0.277): that is not certifier error, it is the mother/child difference. Only Black to Black stands out (certificate 0.18 vs household 0.39), which is paper 3's subject.

**2.2 The decisive comparison is missing from the paper and the paper's own numbers give it.** My arithmetic: put the births through the household flows at age 0 (Brown mothers' births to White 0.277, etc.) to get births by the *child's* classification, and divide the certified deaths by them. White children about 14.5 M, Brown about 14.0 M (births 2013-2023); certified rates about 10.3 and 13.0 per 1,000, ratio about 0.79; this agrees with the modelled census-based 0.80. So the reversal 0.81 to 1.31 is almost entirely numerator and denominator on different constructs (child vs mother). The paper should say this plainly: the "usual indicator" is not biased by the certifier so much as it divides child-race deaths by mother-race births. That is a different, sharper message than "the classification of deaths", and it also dissolves the Discussion's "misclassification" arithmetic. For Black to Brown the same calculation gives about 0.80 (certified over household-flowed births), against 1.15 by the mother and 1.19 by the family column (in which deaths are passed through the flows too). The certificate under-uses "Black" beyond what households do; there the certifier effect is real and the paper does not separate it.

**2.3 The four "inequalities" are not four of one thing.** Two cells vary the numerator (mother, certificate) and two the denominator (births, census population), plus a fifth composite (family). A 2 x 2 design (numerator: mother-classified or certified; denominator: mother's race, household-classified child) with the family column as the consistent-child cell would answer the question cleanly. Currently the census-based column is a modelled population (S2) with no interval and an unexplained jump between infancy (0.80) and 1-4 (0.94), while the household classification gives 0.79 at the same age; the text concedes the account may be wrong but still prints it in the Abstract.

**2.4 Mother's race as reference.** Not a truth; staff-entered (paper 3: hospitals record 0.36 of self-declared Black mothers as Black). The paper acknowledges this once in the Limitations. The consequence should be quantified, not named: Black mothers recorded Brown at birth enter the Brown denominator and their deaths the Brown numerator, so the linked Black to Brown 1.15 is itself attenuated. Show the sensitivity of the mother's-race ratios to the net flows measured in paper 3 (Black-declared recorded Black at 0.36; net 0.74).

**2.5 Denominators.** Births per year, deaths of the same calendar years (infant deaths of 2013 include births of 2012; period against cohort), SIM and SINASC coverage by race and region (North/Northeast under-registration correlates with Brown/Indigenous, and race missingness correlates with both). No coverage adjustment, no bounds. Certified rates drop deaths with no race (about 8% of deaths); mother's-race rates keep them via linkage: the asymmetry alone depresses every certified rate. The sensitivity (1.31 to 1.22) is given for White to Brown only and not for Black to Brown, where it matters most.

**2.6 The estimator after infancy.** At 1-4 y the share of deaths found among candidates is 0.70-0.77 (vs 0.87-0.93 in infancy; `results.json`), and the assumption that the found share does not differ by race cannot be tested after infancy, where families move more (the estimator paper itself shows coverage falling to 0% when moving depends on race). The sensitivity of 10 points is a single scenario. For 5-9 y, only cohorts of 2012-2019 enter. The 1-4 and 5-9 mother's ratios are weakly identified (0.73 (0.67; 0.80); 0.72 (0.60; 0.85)) and carry no linkage-based benchmark: do not give them the same weight as the linked infant contrast, and keep them in the Abstract only as secondary.

## 3. Analyses the data allow but the paper lacks (ranked)

1. **Counterfactual and decomposition through the flow matrix (all in hand).** Decompose the move from 0.81 to 1.31 into: Brown to White deaths, White to Brown, Black to Brown, no-race deaths, and the denominator (mother vs child). A waterfall of ratio steps (one flow switched on at a time, then in the Shapley order). Add the 2 x 2 of 2.3 including certified deaths over household-classified births. Then region by region (flows by region exist in `decomposition`/census `by_region`). This is the paper's real contribution and costs one table.
2. **Region-standardised and adjusted national ratios.** Mantel-Haenszel or Poisson with region (or state-year) fixed effects, for each classification, with maternal schooling, age, parity (SINASC fields are on the linked records) as an adjusted secondary model. The crude national figure is confounded (1.5). Add a 95% interval that carries the linkage uncertainty (bootstrap over linkage draws).
3. **Neonatal (0-27 d) and post-neonatal ratios, and by cause.** Age at death (SIM `IDADE`) and underlying cause (`CAUSABAS`) are on every infant record. Is the reversal the same for early neonatal, congenital, infectious, external, ill-defined deaths? Certification practice differs by cause and by who certifies (SVO/IML vs attending physician, paper 3), so the certified ratio will vary by cause: a straightforward test of whether the departure is a certification feature. Also leave out SVO/IML-certified deaths.
4. **Time trends 2013-2023 of all four ratios.** Recording quality of the mother's race changed (2012 fill 92.7%), the Black category rises on certificates (+0.42 log-odds a decade in paper 3), COVID years differ. A year-by-year figure with intervals shows whether the reversal is stable, shrinking or widening, and that it is not an artefact of a particular year. Linked data support it directly.
5. **Official comparator.** Reproduce the Ministry's published infant mortality by race (SIM and SINASC through TabNet/Painel de Monitoramento da Mortalidade Infantil e Fetal), or state precisely what it publishes (verify; I did not). The paper's "usual indicator" is defined by the authors; if the Ministry's number is the one in daily use, show that it equals the paper's certified-over-births (1.31, 0.49) and add the state-level published values as an external check on the national and regional certified column.
6. **Birth weight, gestational age, plurality** (on the linked records): is the Brown/White ratio under the mother's classification explained by birth weight (mediator) or by place? A stratified table (low birth weight, term) would show whether the "excess" is within-weight, and whether the certified reversal holds within strata (it should not depend on weight).
7. **Indigenous.** Largest ratios (2.48 certified, 1.60 mother's; 4.9 and 6.0 at 1-4 y) and the lowest linkage (61.9%): the Indigenous mother's ratio is the most biased by the global scale (1.2). Redo with certified-race-specific scale, add the linkage of Indigenous infants' municipality (villages, SESAI deaths often outside municipal registries), and report with caveat; do not leave it unmentioned in the Discussion.
8. **Municipal size, urban/rural, state.** The ratio of ratios by municipal size (the estimator itself fails above 25,600 births a year) and by state (S2 gives 1-9 y only). Infants by state with the linkage: 27 rows, no model required.
9. **Inequality measures beyond ratios.** Rate differences (per 1,000), population-attributable deaths ("how many infant deaths would be averted if Brown and Black mothers had White mothers' rates", under each classification), and a relative index of inequality across the race order. Differences are less sensitive to the numerator/denominator problem than ratios when the rates are all moved; the contrast of difference and ratio under each classification would be informative. Absolute difference by the mother: Black minus White 4.6 per 1,000, the figure that matters for policy and is not given.
10. **Black to Brown by region.** In the objective, absent from the regional layer. The infant ratio reverses by a factor of 2.4, more than White to Brown; map it or state why not.
11. **Posterior predictive and sensitivity of the BYM2 to the prior** (see 5).

## 4. Rival explanations

Named but untested, or unnamed:
- **Child's race differs from mother's race legitimately** (mixed parentage; the household flows show it). Test: 2.2 above. Result is already almost determined by the paper's data (0.79).
- **Differential coverage and missingness**: SIM under-registration and no-race certificates by region/race; SINASC race blank by region. Tests: bounds with a plausible range of coverage by region, the no-race sensitivity for all ratios, restricting to regions with coverage over 90%.
- **Composition (Simpson)**: crude vs region-standardised ratios (1.5, 3.2).
- **Linkage selection**: unlinked deaths (19.5%; 38% for Indigenous) are home deaths, deaths in other municipalities, missing birth weight; the weights by certified race do not capture the mother's race of the unlinked. A bound (all unlinked White; all unlinked Brown) is cheap and should be reported; a tipping-point is better than the assertion "cannot be checked".
- **False links.** 0.57-0.75% overall, but not by race; a false link attaches a random mother's race, biasing toward the majority (Brown). Estimate false links by mother's race.
- **Mother's race recorded after a bad outcome / by hospital**: the birth record is entered by staff at birth, not affected by death, which is a strength; but the mother's classification of the Black category (paper 3, net 0.74) attenuates Black to Brown.
- **Place of birth vs place of residence/death**: the region's rates use residence; deaths in hospital in a different region of a rare-White region inflate the certified White term (paper 1: certification depends on place of death). The ratio of ratios highest where White births are rare (Fig. 2D) is by construction partly this; call it arithmetic, as the Discussion says, and do not read it as a finding.
- **Prior-driven maps**: Figure 2A is nearly uniform; this is the shrinkage of a model with 2 observations and 2 random effects per region, not a finding of homogeneity.

## 5. Statistical issues

5.1 **No adjustment** anywhere (region, year, maternal age, schooling, plurality). The crude national ratio is a mixture over regions (1.5). Report adjusted and unadjusted.
5.2 **Intervals**: certified ratio Poisson only (1.1); mother's ratio uses state jackknife but not linkage error, scale uncertainty or false links; census-based and family ratios have none. A paper whose headline is a difference between ratios needs an interval for the difference of log ratios, computed jointly (bootstrap resampling deaths/births by region, redoing linkage scaling).
5.3 **Regional models**: two data rows per region with a region effect and a region-by-White contrast effect, both BYM2: a saturated structure whose posterior is mostly prior (mother's sd_total 0.16, phi 0.72; the national term 0.90 pulls every region). No overdispersion term, no prior sensitivity (PC prior parameters are not given), no posterior predictive check, no exceedance probabilities, no multiplicity control for 510 regional claims, Gaussian approximation `empirical Bayes` (hyperparameters at the mode, understating uncertainty). The two classifications are fitted separately (1.4). Scaled non-integer counts (1.2). Recommended: one joint model of the 4 x 4 flow (multinomial of the certified class given mother's class by region), with regional ratios as derived quantities; at minimum compare with raw regional ratios and report which regions have enough White deaths for the mother's ratio to be data-driven (the count of regions with at least 20 White deaths).
5.4 **Credibility rule** (mean +/- 1.96 sd) with 510 regions: about 13 regions would exclude 1 by chance at the null; state the false-discovery view or use probability of direction.
5.5 **Class means by composition** (3.15, 1.92, 1.83, 2.19): hand-set cut-points (10, 30, 60%), no intervals, ratio-of-ratios averaged on the log scale and weighted by births. The WLS in `spatial.json` has R-squared 0.065: composition and GDP explain 6.5% of the ratio of ratios, which the paper does not say; Figure 2D's smooth is a spline of 4-8 points. The "highest where White births are rare" statement is fine; the rise above 60% is not supported.
5.6 **Multiplicity and primary analysis**: the declared primary contrast (infant White to Brown, mother vs certified) is a descriptive difference of two rates on the same deaths with no null hypothesis; calling it "primary" has no inferential meaning. State that the paper is descriptive.
5.7 **Linked ratio CI** 0.78; 0.84 is from 27 states; the leave-one-out range (0.806-0.820) is far narrower than the interval, which suggests the jackknife se is dominated by one or two states; report which.
5.8 **Table 1 mixes denominators of different periods** (2013-2023, 2013-2024, 2016-2024, cohorts 2012-2019) in one table; one column per period.

## 6. Claims stronger than the evidence

1. "Brown children's excess mortality ... are reversed by the usual infant indicator" (Abstract, Conclusion): conflates mother and child (1.3, 2.1); "reversal" implies the certified ratio is wrong, when for White to Brown it is the right ratio of a different quantity (2.2).
2. "The usual infant indicator": the paper defines it; no source shows that official statistics compute it so (3.5).
3. "Infant and child mortality by race should be computed from linked records" (Abstract): linked records give the *mother's* race, itself a staff-entered classification; whether it is the better target is argued, not shown. A reasonable recommendation is to report both, with denominators named, as the paper's last sentence nearly says.
4. "Direction did not depend on the choices of the estimator" (Results): at 1-4 y the interval is 0.54-0.81 and the sensitivity range 0.57-0.77: direction holds, with an interval whose upper end touches 0.81, and with no benchmark after infancy.
5. "The census-based ratio is near 1 at 1-4 y" is based on a modelled account without an interval, an unexplained break between ages, and a contradiction with the family column; remove from the Abstract or give a range (0.79-0.94).
6. The class-means-by-composition and the 446-vs-159 asymmetry are artefacts of model structure and prior (5.3, 5.5); present as such.
7. "A linked share that differed by less than one point ... so the primary contrast does not rest on the estimator": linkage equality by *certified* race is not equality by *mother's* race (the exposure), and Indigenous 61.9% is the counter-example.
8. Title says "four inequalities"; the paper estimates four ratios of the same two groups, not four inequalities (rephrase "four ratios"). "Racial inequality in child mortality" is stronger than "ratios of rates by the mother's classification".

## 7. Presentation

- Table 1 is dense (four classes, six columns, intervals in every cell); split into main (White to Brown and Black to Brown, infants and 1-4 y) and a supplementary table; give counts of deaths and births per cell, which a referee needs and which are absent. Add the linked share and numbers of deaths by class.
- Figure 1: the y-axis of D is categorical with ragged vertical offsets; the right-most panel mixes interval and non-interval markers; add the missing intervals or hatch the no-interval markers; panel A's black Black line makes the Black reversal (15.6 to 5.2) the visual headline while the abstract headlines White to Brown; choose one.
- Figure 2: A is almost uniform (prior). Replace with raw regional ratios for regions with at least 20 White deaths or exceedance probability maps; C and D largely repeat paper 1's flow maps (place paper). Colour censored at 4 while values reach 12.6.
- The Abstract states 14 numbers; reduce to the primary contrast, the child-race comparator (2.2) and the regional counts.
- "Ratio of ratios" and "reversal" are used for the same object; define once. "Mother's race" and "certified race" should be "birth-record (mother's) race" and "certificate (child's) race".
- Methods: state software versions and priors in the supplement; RECORD checklist missing (audit 1).
- Table S1: the "Ilhéus ¿ Itabuna" encoding is broken; "certified over mother's" column of ratios is fine, but regions with raw White death counts below 20 should be flagged. Sort by births weight too.
- Supplement S2 is one paragraph for a result printed in the Abstract; if census-based ratios stay, give the shares of children of each race by year used, and the 2022 vs 2010 inconsistency.

## 8. Overlap with siblings

- **Paper 1 (place)**: the flow table (0.25, 0.55, 0.17), the linkage, the regional BYM2 maps of the certified-White probability among Brown deaths, and the rise of the flow with White share are paper 1's results. Fig. 2C-D and the Discussion mechanism repeat them. Keep here only what is new: the effect on rates and ratios.
- **Paper 3 (black category)**: the Black to Brown reversal (the largest, 0.49 vs 1.15) is the consequence of paper 3's finding (net 0.34 on certificates, 0.74 in hospital records). Cross-cite; do not explain the mechanism here, and do not leave Black to Brown out of the regional analysis while paper 3 covers the cause.
- **Paper 6 (estimator)**: the 25,600 threshold, the t differential and the simulation belong there. The estimator's own text says that unequal moving by race biases the estimate and that coverage fell to 0%; paper 2 relies on it for 1-9 y without citing that failure mode. Contradiction risk: paper 6's validation shows White to White candidate 0.689 vs linkage 0.715 in 6,400-25,599; paper 2 retains that stratum.
- **Unreleased paper (family)**: the household flows used in the "family's classification" column are its results; paper 2 needs them in a table of its own (2.2) and a citation.
- Numbers shared across papers (0.25/0.55/0.17 flows; linked share 80.5%; false links) should come from one source cell to avoid drift; the flows have confidence intervals in paper 1 and none here.
