Supplementary material - Race on the death certificate and place: the mother's declaration, the family's classification and the certified race of infants and children across 5,570 Brazilian municipalities, 2013-2024

Supplementary Text S1 - Estimation without individual linkage and its control

Let G be a group of deaths. For a death i, let C_i be the set of live births with the birth date, sex and municipality of residence of i. If the family did not move, exactly one element of C_i is the deceased; the remaining elements, and all elements when the family moved, are births that agree by chance. Write N(j, k) for the number of deaths in G with race j on the birth record and certified race k, and t for the probability that the deceased is in C_i. The expected number of candidates with race j among deaths certified k is t N(j, k) + E(j, k), where E(j, k) is the expected number of chance candidates.

E(j, k) depends on how many children were born on the birth date of the deceased in the municipality. That is not the number of an arbitrary day: a day on which more children were born contributes more people and therefore more deaths, so the birth date of a death is a size-biased draw from the days of the year, and a control drawn from other days would undercount E. The births of the other sex on the same day in the same municipality share the day, cannot contain the deceased and have the racial composition of that day's births; multiplied by the ratio of births of the two sexes in the municipality and year, their count estimates E(j, k). With this control the estimated t for infants was 89.8% overall (Supplementary Table S4).

The estimator of t N(j, k) is the difference of the two counts. Its variance was approximated by the sum of the candidate count and the control count, which ignores the scaling factor of the control (close to 1). A proportion is a ratio of two such differences; the variance of its denominator was ignored, which understates the error of rows with few deaths. A twin of the same sex is a candidate and a twin of the other sex is in the control; both share the mother's race with the deceased. Mortality by the mother's race uses the shares of each race among the estimated deaths, so that t cancels provided it does not differ by race.

Supplementary Table S10 - Weight of the local distribution (λ) in a mixture model of the certified race of children, by mother's race, age and reference distribution, Brazil

C(m -> c) = (1 - λ) F(m -> c) + λ π(c) over ten classes of municipal composition, F the family's classification in the census and π the local reference. λ = 0: the certificate records the family's classification; λ = 1: it records the local distribution whatever the mother's race. With the certified race of local deaths as the reference, the reference contains the certificates being modelled, which biases λ upward; the fit for Black mothers is poor under both references.
