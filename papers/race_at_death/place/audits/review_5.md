# Review 5: "Race on the death certificate and place" (place/out/manuscript.txt, supplement.txt)

Reviewer stance: IJE / AJE / Lancet Reg Health. Read: both texts in full, figures (between_within.png seen; others via captions), results/spatial/panel JSON keys, analysis code (skim), audits 1-4 and response 4, abstracts of the three sibling papers, `pegasus-data info` and the manual (columns, links, fields). Points already answered in responses 1-4 are not repeated.

## 0. Overall judgement

**Major revision.** Descriptive core is strong and novel: a linked, national, 11-year comparison of the certified race of infants with the mother's declaration, with a census reference, and a clear spatial gradient. The linkage is well documented (false-link control, held-out fields). The question in the title, however, is causal-mechanistic ("a classification of place?", "what explains it"). The evidence answers a weaker one: the certificate-vs-mother disagreement is strongly structured by municipality and state, and municipal wealth/services add little. The paper then reads this as "a third classification whose relation depends on place", and says it is "consistent with" a certifier who draws on the local population. That reading is not tested against its two nearest rivals (copying from the birth record; the Brown category differing by region). Four ecological designs (spline deviance, border test, Mundlak, long-difference) give the same ecological answer. None uses the individual-level variables the data hold to separate *who* classifies. The decisive designs (place of residence vs place of death vs establishment; same-establishment copy from the DN; symmetric cells; adult/IML placebo; census 2000/2010/2022 long panel) are all feasible with data on the machine and absent. The within-place result is under-powered and oversold. The paper is ~9,900 words as extracted (the brief says ~7,100; abstract plus tables of CIs inflate it) and carries material that belongs in sibling papers.

## 1. Does the design answer the question?

What "classification of place" would require. Three claims are distinct and the paper merges them:
1. Disagreement (certificate vs mother) is geographically structured. Shown, convincingly.
2. The classifier's output is a function of local composition (a norm or conformity). Shown only as a between-municipality association, with composition measured from the same record system (SINASC) that supplies the reference.
3. The classifier is an institution or person who applies a local or state practice (a causal mechanism). Not identified: the certificate does not record the informant, and every contrast is across places.

For claim 2 or 3 one needs at least one of: (a) variation in the classifier holding the child and family fixed; (b) variation in composition holding the classifier fixed; (c) a dose-response in how much the classifier could have known the family. The paper has none of these. The establishment random effect (variance 0.29 vs 0.03 at municipality) is the closest to (a), but it is unlabelled variance (hospital catchment composition is unmeasured, as the authors say) and the paper states it does not partition.

What the evidence rules out (fairly): pure transmission of the mother's race; a pure copy of the family's classification (Discussion, nationally equal for Brown→White but regionally opposite); wealth/services as the main driver; case-mix (-1%). What it does not rule out: (i) the certifier copies the DN or hospital chart (a transmission with a place-dependent error), (ii) the Brown category is regionally heterogeneous, (iii) the reference (SINASC mother's race) is itself place-dependent, producing the gradient mechanically in the "certified minus mother" contrast. The paper names (ii) and (iii) in limitations but does not test them. Statement "Two explanations remain open" is the honest sentence, but the Abstract and Conclusion do not carry it.

Unfalsifiable third reading. "Reflects a classification made by whoever fills it" is a residual category, not a hypothesis. It should be split into (3a) conformity to local majority, (3b) state administrative practice, (3c) social position, each with a sign prediction on cells the paper does not map (see section 3). Without that, "the data are consistent with the third reading" cannot lose.

Inference at the level of the certificate versus mother. The title framing is "place", but the infant result is a statement about Brown→White (and Black→Brown). A "local majority" account predicts darkening of White mothers' infants in the Northeast and Black→Brown wherever Brown predominate. Those cells (White mothers' infants certified Brown; Black mothers' infants certified White/Brown by composition of Brown) are only in Supplementary Figure S3 as maps; there is no formal test of symmetry. The 35% crossing point (Fig 3C) is reported without uncertainty and is not compared with the predicted crossing under a majority rule (if the certificate followed the local majority of mothers, Brown mothers' infants would be certified White only where White is the majority: the crossing would be ~50%, not 35%).

## 2. Analyses the data allow but the paper lacks (ranked by effect on conclusions)

Data checked in the manual: SIM-DOINF has CODMUNRES, CODMUNOCOR, CODESTAB, ATESTANTE, LOCOCOR, CIRCOBITO, ESCMAE; SINASC-DN has CODESTAB (birth establishment), LOCNASC, CONSPRENAT, ESCMAE2010, RACACORMAE, and RACACORN (newborn colour in the 2012-2013 files); SIH-RD has RACA_COR plus links `sih_neonatal_admissions_to_sinasc`, `sih_deaths_to_sim`, `sinasc_births_to_delivery_admission`; fields `population_white`, `population_indigenous`, `population_race_universe` for census 2000, 2010, 2022; SIDRA 9578/8176 (quilombola 2022); SIM adult deaths 1996-2024 with RACACOR.

1. **Copy-from-DN test (same-establishment, early-neonatal).** Source: the linked pairs (SIM x SINASC). Specification: indicator S = death in the establishment where the child was born (SIM.CODESTAB = SINASC.CODESTAB) and, within S, age at death 0-1 day. Fit Pr(certified = mother's race) by S, age, mother's race, composition spline, state, municipality RE. Prediction: if the certifier copies the DN, agreement should be near 1 in S-early deaths and fall with distance from the birth record (home deaths, other-municipality deaths, deaths after 28 days); the gradient with composition should then live in the *disagreement* of the DN with the mother, i.e. in the birth record, not the certificate. Add the SINASC newborn-colour disagreement (RACACORN vs RACACORMAE, 2012-2013 files: both fields on one record) as a direct measure of how place-dependent the birth record's own classification is, and use it to put an error bar around the reference. This is the single analysis that decides whether the "third classification" exists at all or is the second classification (DN) mis-copied.
2. **Residence vs occurrence vs establishment (mobility design).** Source: SIM CODMUNRES, CODMUNOCOR, CODESTAB. Among deaths occurring outside the residence municipality (referral to regional hospitals, a large share in interior states), regress certified race on composition of residence and composition of occurrence municipality (and of the establishment's catchment, via its own births), with mother's race x each. Residence composition measures the family and informant; occurrence composition and establishment measure the certifier. The paper's present "municipality of residence" gradient confounds these; the state border test is a weak proxy. Add: the establishment random effect using only referral deaths (the establishment is then not the family's place).
3. **Long panel with the census: 2000/2010/2022 and SIM since 1996.** Source: SIM certified race of all infant deaths by municipality-year (no linkage needed) 1996-2024; census race shares by municipality in 2000, 2010 and 2022 (`population_white`, universe field). The Mundlak panel of 2013-2023 has a within SD of 0.35 and is dominated by sampling noise (41%) and by a secular reclassification that the year effects remove. A 1996-2024 panel with three census anchors has a within variation two orders larger (Brazil lost 7 points of White share 2010-2022) and a pre-2011 period when the birth record was the newborn's colour, an observer classification. Specification: municipality fixed effects, outcome logit(share certified White among all infant deaths), exposure interpolated census logit(share White), clustered by immediate region; a placebo with a municipality's future composition (lead). This is the proper answer to "behaves as a trait of the place" and to the (-0.05; 0.63) interval, which currently supports neither "a trait" nor "a modest response".
4. **Dose-response by certifier knowledge (placebo/gradient by type).** Source: ATESTANTE, CIRCOBITO, LOCOCOR. Add composition x certifier-type interactions (IML for external causes, SVO, attending), x age (neonatal vs post-neonatal), x death at home vs hospital, x mother's schooling and age. Prediction table in the paper: administrative practice predicts equal gradient everywhere; informant-based classification predicts a weaker gradient where no family interview is possible (IML external deaths, SVO). Currently the paper reports only main effects of certifier (OR 1.58) and no slope heterogeneity. Add an adult-death placebo: the same municipal gradient for adults 20-59 certified race against census composition (SIM 1996-2024) separates "child-specific informant" from "general certifier practice". Reference the adult/child gap.
5. **Symmetric cells and a full multinomial.** Fit a multinomial (hierarchical) model of certified race (W/B/Bk/I) given mother's race x composition x state; test the pre-specified majority-conformity predictions: slope of White mothers' infants certified non-White on (1 - composition); crossing point with its CI; Black mothers' infants certified Brown vs Brown-share. Only Brown→White is carried through the whole machinery (BYM2, borders, multilevel, Mundlak). The Black-category analysis is summary only.
6. **Individual-level heterogeneity of the Brown category (the main rival).** Source: census 2010 microdata (already in hand): per municipality, the distribution of Brown mothers' children's family classification conditional on mother's schooling, age, urban residence, and (from the household roster) the presence and recorded race of the partner. Build a family-expected P(White) per municipality-year and ask whether the certificate minus the family-expected still has the gradient. Currently the family benchmark is not adjusted for the covariates the paper itself shows move the certificate (mother's schooling 12+: OR 1.73). Also add SINASC-side covariates already used to link: mother's age, schooling, prenatal visits (CONSPRENAT), LOCNASC; present the individual-level cross-level interaction (composition x mother's schooling), which would tell whether "social position" works inside the "composition" gradient.
7. **Linkage selection by place.** 19.5% of deaths are unlinked, 24% in the North; unlinked deaths have only the certificate. Inverse-probability weights from linkage-correlates (region, age, certified race, municipality size) or Manski-type bounds on the map. Provide the spatial map of the linked share and test whether it correlates with composition (it would bias any composition coefficient if linkage fails differentially by mother's race; Indigenous 61.9% already shows it can).
8. **Hospital chart of the child (SIH) as a third measurement of the same child.** Source: `sih_neonatal_admissions_to_sinasc` and `sih_deaths_to_sim`: RACA_COR in the hospital record of infants who died (or were admitted before). If the hospital record of the child already follows composition as steeply, the gradient belongs to the observer rather than to the certificate; if not, to the certificate. The sibling paper uses the mother's delivery admission; the child's own record is not used.
9. **Reference calibration.** Compare the SINASC mother's race shares by municipality with the census 2022 race shares of women 15-49 (SIDRA has race by age/sex tables for 2022; check) to estimate place-dependence of the DN reference and put a measurement-error bound on the "disagreement" map. Use also the 2012 form transition (2.5%/55%/92.7% filled) as a natural experiment for DN practice by place.
10. **Mortality-selection and denominators.** Disagreement among deaths is not disagreement among births. If death risk differs by mother's race within municipality, the composition of deaths differs from that of births; the paper maps Brown mothers' deaths only, so this is mostly benign, but a sentence and a sensitivity analysis (reweight deaths to births by cause) is cheap.
11. Smaller: SIM fetal and maternal deaths (DOFET, DOMAT) as a second certificate type with the same certifiers; sub-municipal Indigenous and quilombola territories (SIDRA 9578) as a case where the "norm" is known to differ; SIA/SIH utilisation as service proxy beyond physician density (the "services" block is weak: physicians per 1,000, verification-service share).

## 4. Statistical issues

1. Ecological association is carried to a causal verb ("explained 71% of deviance"). Deviance explained is a descriptive overlap, and the F-tests are acknowledged invalid (residual Moran 0.15-0.71). Report instead a model with spatial random effect plus fixed composition and state (BYM2 + covariates in INLA) and the posterior of the fixed coefficients; deal with spatial confounding (Hodges-Reich; consider restricted spatial regression) because composition is itself a smooth spatial field (Moran's I ~0.8), so the state/composition split is partly arbitrary. The share "composition 71%, state 75%, both 81%" cannot be read as separate contributions.
2. Composition and outcome share the measurement source (SINASC mother's race). Substituting the census (r = 0.94) is a good check, but only for deviance, not for the Mundlak and the multilevel; the census interpolation for years between censuses is exactly what is needed (item 3).
3. Mundlak: (a) the "corrected for noise" within coefficient (16% / 21%) is not described in Methods (reliability ratio? how estimated?); (b) a random-intercept binomial with nAGQ=0 on municipality-years with many cells of 0-3 deaths is unreliable for variance components; (c) with n(deaths) as exposure, weights favour large municipalities; (d) year effects remove the national trend, which is where most within-variation in composition lives; (e) the only within covariates that vary (no-race share, SVO share) are themselves outcomes of the same certification process: -1.54 on "proportion with no race" is a mechanical link (a certificate with no race is not a certificate with White), so it should not be interpreted. (f) State-by-year effects or at least region-by-year effects are needed for the within coefficient to be about the municipality and not about state policies that vary in time.
4. Uncertainty. Tables report binomial 95%CI of 0.25 (0.25; 0.25) in Table 2 while the text gives bootstrap (0.23; 0.27) for the same quantity; unify (use the cluster interval throughout; the abstract does). The CIs for the candidate estimator exceed [0,1] (-0.06 (-0.20; 0.07), 1.01 (0.84; 1.19)): do not print them as a main table; move to supplement with a delta-method or bootstrap. The Poisson variance for the estimator is declared an understatement by the sibling estimator paper (SE 0.034 vs 0.023); use the jackknife in this paper too. Moran and BYM variance ratios (4.9x) have no interval; give the posterior interval of the ratio. Share "5 times" is a ratio of variances of smoothed estimates and depends on shrinkage; the paper argues it does not; show it with both fitted jointly (shared hyperprior) and the posterior of the ratio.
5. Multiplicity. Seven exploratory contrast blocks, two cells, three ages, local Moran at p < 0.05 for 3,459 units without FDR: apply FDR (or at least a Bonferroni-LISA/Benjamini-Hochberg) and report the number surviving; presented "546 clusters of high values" is meaningless without it.
6. Multilevel: nAGQ=0 PQL for binary outcomes with sparse clusters understates variance; refit with Laplace (nAGQ=1) or a Bayesian GLMM at least for the key models; report the median odds ratio with interval. "Proportion of variance explained" after adding individual variables is not comparable (acknowledged); use the Merlo MOR/IOR or the Raudenbush-Bryk scale-fixed approach or report the variance with the latent scale fixed through a probit comparison.
7. Ecological inference. The family's classification is of other children, 2010; the paper uses the ratio of variances (1.38 vs 0.28) as the centrepiece. Different periods (2010 vs 2013-23), different populations (living children with a household reporter vs deaths), different age (0-9 vs infants), different respondents. The ratio mixes real variation in classification with differences in population heterogeneity; the family variance is also reduced by the age/mother's race pooling. A paired structure (same municipalities, deaths of 2008-2012 vs census 2010 children under 2) would be cleaner, using the birth record's newborn colour in that period.
8. Measurement error in the reference. The DN mother's race is the reference; any place-dependent error in it enters the "disagreement" one-for-one. At minimum a sensitivity analysis: assume a symmetric misclassification rate of mother's race in DN of x% by place and show the gradient's shrinkage (bias analysis, not just a limitation line).
9. After infancy. Estimator validity depends on movers (71.7% found), and the sibling estimator paper shows bias of 13 points in one cell when moving depends on the class and coverage down to 0%; the place paper uses it for a confirmation claim ("The dependence on composition held after infancy") with CIs 0.16-0.29 and 0.54-0.80. Either bound the bias with the sibling paper's simulation or label as supportive only.
10. Sample definitions drift: title 2013-2024; Abstract 2013-2023 infants; linked n 296,512; Moran n 3,459; panel 4,974 municipalities; 1,458 for long-difference. Add a flow figure (STROBE/RECORD item 13).

## 5. Claims stronger than evidence

1. Abstract/Conclusion: "The race on the death certificate of a child is a third classification, distinct from the mother's declaration and the family's". The certificate differing from two references is not evidence of a distinct classification; it could be a transcription of a third record (DN, hospital chart, family informant) with error. Wording: "The race on the death certificate differed from both references and the difference was structured by municipality and state; whether it reflects a separate classification or a copy of another record is untested."
2. "The data are consistent with the third reading, a classification by whoever fills the certificate that is associated with the local population and has a state component." Consistent with is true of almost anything. Wording: "The association with composition and the step at state borders are what a local or state classification norm would produce; they are equally produced by a regionally heterogeneous Brown category and by place-dependent recording on the birth record, neither of which was separated."
3. "Over time the gradient behaved as a trait of the place: ... moved about 16% as far... A norm that responded to the local population as it is would follow its changes." Interval of the census long-difference (-0.05; 0.63) includes full response; within SD 0.35 mostly noise; the place-level "trait" reading and a norm that is slow to adjust are indistinguishable. Wording: "The within-place change was small and imprecise, and the data do not distinguish a fixed trait from a slowly adjusting norm."
4. "The step is small beside the differences between neighbours" and "The certified race changes in a step at state borders, as a state practice would make it". Slope 0.46 (0.26; 0.65): by that estimate 54% of the state difference is *not* at the border; mean squared logit difference 0.56 vs 0.49 across vs within states is a trivial excess. This is more consistent with a smooth gradient plus a modest state component than with a border step; the paper reads it as support for state practice. Wording: "At state borders the certified race moved by about half of the difference between state interiors, implying a state component and a continuous component; the estimate is attenuated by error in the state effects."
5. "wealth, schooling, urbanisation, the supply of physicians and the services that certify accounted for less than composition and little in addition to it". Context variables are crude (mean GDP, physicians per 1,000, share of verification service), and the paper itself shows in the multilevel model a 55% variance share explained by SES alone; "little in addition" is conditional on composition that is itself a function of social position (White places are richer places in Brazil). Wording: "Context added little beyond composition, which is itself collinear with it."
6. "and the characteristics of the deaths themselves accounted for none of it". The variables in the model are cause group, age, certifier, birth weight, schooling; with macro-region they explain 74%, i.e. they matter once region is held; saying none is the result of a rescaled logistic variance. Wording: "did not account for the municipal variation without the macro-region."
7. "Racial composition explained 71% of the deviance between municipalities" (Abstract). Deviance share of a binomial model with dispersion 8.4; a non-causal verb is needed: "was associated with 71% of the deviance".
8. "No study was found that compared..." (Introduction). A priori claim of novelty on a review that is not described; the study of Rebouças et al. and Caldas et al. etc. Reword to "To our knowledge".
9. Implications: "A child's race on the death certificate depends on where the child died, on the state and on the establishment". The design cannot say where the child died (residence is used); an establishment effect is a variance, not a cause. And "linkage ... removes the certificate's dependence on place": the birth record may be as place-dependent (see 2.1, 2.9).
10. "Black category ... depended on the state more than on composition, and rose ... without a clear difference between regions" contradicts the Results: regional slopes with SD 0.47, p = 0.0004, from 0.19 (North) to 0.99 (South) per decade. Fix the Discussion.
11. Abstract: "neighbouring municipalities differed by 0.46 of the difference between their states": reads as a descriptive fact about municipalities; it is a regression slope on estimated state effects; say so.

## 6. Presentation

- Length: the extracted manuscript is ~9,900 words (reference list included ~7,100 body). Seven figures, three tables, 11 supplementary figures: too many main outputs for the question.
- Cut or move: Table 2 (60 rows; keep Brown and Black mothers' infants; the rest to supplement; remove rows outside [0,1]); the 1-9-year section (one paragraph with the caveat of item 4.9, move to supplement or to the estimator paper); the Black-category analyses (Moran 0.42, time slopes by region, Fig 6) belong to the black_category paper; the time analysis by intermediate region (Fig 6) is not about "place explaining the disagreement".
- Restructure the argument as predictions-and-tests: one table of "reading x prediction x result" (the three readings are in the Introduction as prose and the Discussion never returns to a table). Replace the five-part place-explaining sequence with the tests of Section 2 items 1-5.
- Figures: Fig 4 has five panels (A-E) mixing observed associations and models; Fig 7 (between_within) is the most informative figure but shows no uncertainty about the "16%" and mixes regions and municipalities; show composition within-between only, with the long-panel result. Fig 3 (maps): add the unsmoothed counts as small multiples with a sample-size mask (most municipalities have under 8 deaths of Brown mothers); consider a bivariate map of certificate vs family. Give BYM2 mixing 0.99 as a parameter, not as a result.
- Methods precede results in too much detail (INLA priors, PQL) while the reader never learns what is known about the DN's own filling process. Put the DN field history (S1) in the main text because it is the reference.
- Text defects: the Discussion repeats the abstract; "Two explanations remain open" appears in the middle of the Discussion and not in the Abstract; the introduction's three readings use terms ("classification made by whoever fills it") not mirrored in the Results. Title says 2013-2024, but infants end in 2023.
- Pre-specification: Methods says "No hypothesis test was pre-registered", while the Introduction states predictions; a consistent honest statement would be "the predictions were written after the data had been explored".

## 7. Overlap with sibling papers

- black_category (4,374 words): reports the same cell (0.18 / 0.55 / 0.18 for Black mothers' infants), the rise from 0.15 to 0.21, the multilevel with certifier, the place of the Black category. The place paper repeats these, plus the Moran of the Black category, the Black-category time slope and the establishment variance for the Black outcome. Pick one home for the Black category; in the place paper keep one sentence.
- estimator (5,571 words): the candidate estimator, its validation by the linkage (Supplementary Figure S1, Table S4, Text S1) and the 25,600-birth cut-off are described here as methods, and appear in full in the estimator paper, which also documents a bias the place paper does not cite (moving depending on class: 13 points in the admission cell; coverage down to 0%). Cross-reference and cut; consider moving the 1-9-year section to the estimator or indicators paper.
- indicators (3,778 words): the White-to-Brown and Black-to-Brown infant ratio by mother's race against certified race. The place paper only mentions "a companion analysis"; the abstract of indicators is the policy-relevant consequence and shares the linked-pairs and the regional map (INLA over 510 immediate regions), duplicating the spatial analysis.
- Salami risk: one linkage and one table (mother x certified race) yield four papers with three sets of the same estimates, the same BYM2 machinery and the same multilevel model. An editor will ask for a single statement of what each paper adds. Place: the spatial structure and its covariates. The place paper should drop the Black-category and after-infancy blocks and absorb the new identification designs of Section 2 so that its contribution is a mechanistic test, not a second descriptive set.
- Contradictions to fix: place says the Black category "rose ... without a clear difference between regions" (Discussion) vs regional SD 0.47, p = 0.0004 (Results); black_category says the rise is "faster than in the hospital", the place paper does not use this; check the figure "0.55 certified Brown" and ratio denominators are identical across the four texts (same linkage freeze, same CIs: bootstrap vs binomial).

## Top 5 required changes

1. Test copy-from-DN (same-establishment, early-neonatal agreement; RACACORN vs RACACORMAE), and reframe "third classification" accordingly.
2. Residence vs occurrence vs establishment design (referral deaths), plus composition x certifier-type/age slopes and an adult-death placebo.
3. Replace the 2013-2023 Mundlak by a long panel (SIM 1996-2024 with census 2000/2010/2022) with leads, region-by-year effects; temper the "trait of place" claim.
4. Pre-state predictions for the three readings including symmetric cells and a multinomial model; adjust the family benchmark for the individual covariates; fix the border-test interpretation (slope 0.46 means half is smooth).
5. Cut to one question: remove Black-category and after-infancy blocks, move estimator material out, FDR for local Moran, Laplace refits, spatial-confounding-aware models, one set of intervals throughout.
