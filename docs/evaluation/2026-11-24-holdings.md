# One model of holdings: the extracts against the lake (2026-11-24)

**Why.** ADR-0236 replaces the per-filter extract folders with one store per dataset and a holding per file. Its
safety rests on three checks:
- the rows the old folders hold are the lake's;
- readers get the same tables before and after;
- what the migration would delete is held elsewhere.

**Regime.** The home `pegasus_core_data`, master at bd70ba7c plus the ADR-0236 working tree, 2026-11-24. Scripts and
outputs are under `data/probes/holdings/`: `fingerprint.py`, `before.json`, `after.json`, `compare.py`,
`extract_table_old_vs_new.json`, `migrate_dryrun_*.json`, `migrate_apply_SIA.PA.json`. A table's fingerprint is its row
count, an XOR and a sum of a hash of every row's whole struct, an MD5 of its (`_source_path`, `_row`) sequence in order,
and its schema.

**What the lake held.**
- SIH-SP 2010-2025, every file whole: 4,916 sources in `SIHSUS_SP_c275b49fe5` and 271 in `SIHSUS_SP_3b016b3e09`, the
  2025 layout, built that morning.
- A first count missed that second family and took 270 files of 2025 for unheld.

mismatched and 0 short.

**Migration, SIA-PA** (dry run 1,022 s, then applied).
- 506 files are absorbed by the lake (131,612 rows).
- 5,587 are carried into the store, adding 6,230,880 rows: the rows each adds beyond what an earlier folder holds.
- 390 are short (OQ 112). Their folder recorded 2,765,636 rows that their parts do not hold: split files
  (`PAMG2301a`, `b`, `c`) had written over one another's part.
- MG and RJ 2021-2025, SP 2011-2025, PR and RS 2025 were served short.

**`extract_table`, old code (HEAD) against new**, every column but `_ingested_at`:

| read | rows | identical |
|---|---|---|
| 0403, 2024 | 2,119,682 | rows, values, order, schema |
| 040803, 2025 | 534,328 | rows, values, order, schema |
| 0403, 2015-2016 | 3,120,698 | rows, values, order, schema |

- The old reads came from the folders, the new from the lake. `_ingested_at` is when each copy was decoded, so it
  differs.
- Two defects were found and fixed on the way: the old reads carried a `year` column that pyarrow inferred from the
  part's folder, and their rows were ordered by state, then period.
- From the lake a year of 0403 takes 58 s (one scan of each state-year partition), against about 2 s from the 64 MB
  folder (OQ 109).

**`query("SIH.SP", rows={"SP_PROCREA": [...]}, provenance="all", max_download=0)`, before and after.**
- The reads: 0415 for 2025, 2025-01 and 2025-06 in each of the 27 states, and 0403 for SE 2019 and AC 2024.
- All 83 are identical in every column, `_ingested_at` included: rows, both hashes, order and schema
  (`compare.py`). 2025's 0415 rows nationally are 21,638,613.
- Time was 451 s before and 475 s after.
- Every year is the lake's: a year the lake holds whole was read from it before too, so the extract folders were not
  read.

**Agglutination on real files** (`agglutinate.py`, SIA-PA Acre 2024, 12 files held by the migrated store).
- **A held filter downloads nothing.** `build_extract` of `PA_CIDPRI B58` reads 0 files in 0.6 s.
- **A new filter reads only what is missing.** `PA_PROC_ID 0301010072` reads the 12 files in 22 s and adds 81,401
  rows, each file's third disjoint part. `extract_table` of the new code serves 81,432 rows: 31 were already held
  under the older predicates and were not stored twice.
- **No row is stored twice.** 0 duplicate `_row` across a file's parts.
- **The predicate widens to the union.** The union filter reads 0 files afterwards.
- **An old read is unchanged.** B58 gives 9 rows before and after.

**Tests.** The old offline suite: 1,608 passed, 31 skipped. One failure came first: a catalog without
`lake_partitions.source_digests` made the planner raise. Such a catalog now holds nothing whole.
